Guesstimate 5.1 · Instagram posts a day in India GuesstimateEasyIIM A Product Mindset 2026–27, p. 235
Try it first. How many posts (not Stories) did you put on Instagram last month? Your five closest friends? Is anyone you know posting every day? Build the daily number for India from that, for five minutes.
{{CASE:A235}}
What worked · what was missing
  • Pinned down the unit in three questions: posts only, per day, personal accounts, a typical day.
  • Wrote the equation before the numbers, then filled it step by step.
  • Split users by how often they post, which is exactly the right split for this question.
  • A slip in the maths: heavy users are 5%, so 0.05 × 400 = 20 million; the written “0.5 × 400” would be 200 million. The result used 20, so only the working is wrong, but interviewers notice.
  • The posting rates are high. 60% of users posting every month and 35% every week makes the average Indian user post every 10 days. Most Instagram users post a few times a year.
  • The check was read the wrong way round. India with a third of global posts would mean Indians post about twice as often as everyone else. That should have been a warning, not a confirmation.

Guesstimate kit check: 1 Unit ✓ · 2 Approach ✓ bottom-up by behaviour · 3 Tree ✓ · 4 Numbers with reasons ✗ “from my own experience” · 5 Check ✗ done, but misread · 6 Range ✗. Card 5 needs two checks, and one of them is per person: 38 million ÷ 400 million users = one post every 10½ days per user, which is too often.

Check · “India contributing around a third of the global total is plausible” Doesn’t hold up

India has about 40 crore Instagram users out of roughly 3 billion monthly users worldwide: about 13%. Unless Indians post about 2½ times as often as everyone else, India should contribute something near 13% of global posts, not 38%. And the interviewer’s “100 million posts a day” is an old number from the photo era, before Reels; treat it as a rough anchor, not a fact.

Stronger answer
A
1 · Unit

Feed posts and Reels from personal accounts in India on a typical day. Stories are out; carousels count as one post.

Why this move: “uploads” could include Stories, which are several times more common than posts. Saying what counts protects the answer from a 5× misunderstanding.

A
2 · Approach

Bottom-up from users: monthly active personal accounts, split by how often each group posts, then add up.

Why this move: posting is very uneven, so one average for everyone would hide the few who post most.

A
3–4 · Tree and numbers

140 crore people × 70% online = 98 crore. About 40 crore of them are active on Instagram each month. Split them by behaviour: 70% mostly watch and post about once every two months (28 crore × 1 ÷ 60 ≈ 47 lakh a day); 25% post about every 10 days (10 crore × 0.1 = 1 crore a day); 5% are keen posters and small creators, posting every other day (2 crore × 0.5 = 1 crore a day). Total ≈ 47 lakh + 1 crore + 1 crore ≈ 2.5 crore posts a day ({{REF:gtree51}}).

Why this move: each group gets a rate with a reason you can defend: a watcher posts for birthdays and trips, a regular for weekends, a creator on a schedule.

A
5 · Check

Per person: 2.5 crore ÷ 40 crore users = 0.06 posts a day, one post every 16 days. That sounds right for a mix of watchers and posters. Against the world: if the rest of the world also posts about once every 16–20 days, India’s 13% of users gives about 13–15% of posts. Both checks agree.

Why this move: one check per side, the person and the world, catches most mistakes. This is where the casebook answer would have been corrected.

A
6 · Range

About 1.5 to 4 crore posts a day. The swing factor is the keen posters: at 3% the total falls to about 2 crore; at 8% it rises past 3 crore. With Stories added, the number would be several times higher.

Why this move: naming the swing factor tells the interviewer which assumption to argue with.

How the answer hangs togetherThe unit (posts and Reels, not Stories) fixed what to count. Splitting users by behaviour turned one shaky average into three believable rates, and the per-person and India-share checks both pointed to about 2.5 crore. The range names the one group, keen posters, that moves the answer most.
The reusable trick

Actions a day = active users × share in each behaviour group × how often that group acts. Split by behaviour, then check the answer per person (does one post every 16 days sound like people you know?) and as India’s share of a global number.
