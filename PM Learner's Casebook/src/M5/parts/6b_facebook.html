Facebook and Meta’s ad machine

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Arun runs a bakery in Kochi. Two weeks before Christmas he opens Meta’s ad tool and spends ₹500 a day to show a photo of his plum cakes to adults within 5 km, with a “Send message” button. That evening Priya, 34, scrolls Facebook. In the few milliseconds before the next post appears, Meta holds an auction for that one slot among every advertiser who wants someone like Priya. Arun’s ad wins. Priya taps “Send message”, a WhatsApp chat with the bakery opens, and she orders a 1 kg cake. Meta charges Arun about ₹37 for that message ({{REF:fb-flow}}).

How the auction picks a winner

Meta doesn’t simply show the highest bid. It ranks ads by total value = the advertiser’s bid for an action × the chance that this person does it + a quality score (which falls if people hide or report the ad). Arun bids ₹40 for a message, and Meta predicts a 1.5% chance Priya sends one: ₹40 × 1.5% = ₹0.60, plus a small quality bonus, 0.65. A phone brand bids ₹8 per click at 5%: 0.40, plus 0.10 = 0.50. A loan app bids ₹100 per install at 0.8%: 0.80, but its ads are often reported, so its quality score is −0.20: 0.60 ({{REF:auction}}). Arun wins with 0.65, and pays only enough to beat the next ad, 0.60, which works out to about ₹37 per message instead of his ₹40 bid.

Why design it this way? If the biggest bidder always won, feeds would fill with ads people hate, they would scroll less, and every advertiser would lose. Weighting by the chance of action makes a small bakery competitive against a national brand for the people nearby who might actually want cake. It is also why Meta’s better AI models raise its price per ad: when ads convert better, advertisers bid more.

Keeping the feed safe: the moderation pipeline

Billions of posts a day can’t all be checked by people. Automatic classifiers score each post; posts that clearly break the rules are removed at once, clear cases pass, and uncertain ones go to human reviewers, who work in many Indian languages ({{REF:moder}}). India’s IT Rules 2021 make large platforms publish monthly compliance reports and act on government and court orders within fixed times. The sum that sets the size of the review team: if 2 lakh posts a day in India need a human look, and a reviewer handles about 400 a day, Meta needs 2,00,000 ÷ 400 = 500 reviewers on duty, about 700 after shifts and leave (illustrative).

Business model, with numbers

Meta’s revenue in April–June 2026 was $60.8 bn, up 28%, almost all from ads across Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and WhatsApp. 3.60 bn people used at least one Meta app each day. Its automated campaign tool, Advantage+, runs at about $75 bn a year. Meta also spends heavily: Reality Labs (glasses and headsets) lost $4.6 bn that quarter, and it plans $130–145 bn of spending on data centres for AI in 2026.

Moat, in plain words. Three billion people and the data on what they respond to, plus an auction that small businesses can use for ₹500 a day. Advertisers go where the customers and the results are. The risk is age: Facebook’s users are getting older, which is why the young go to Instagram.

Strengths

  • The largest audience in the world
  • An ad system small shops can use
  • Groups and Marketplace give daily reasons to visit

Weaknesses

  • Ageing users; weak with under-25s
  • Scams on Marketplace and in ads
  • Constant rulings on content and data, in India and abroad
