Guesstimates: counting what people do

Social and messaging guesstimates ask how often people do something: post, send, share, call. The trap is to treat every user as the same person. A few people post daily; most never do. A teenager sends hundreds of messages; her grandfather sends five. Split users by behaviour, and anchor the answer to a published total. The six cards still do the work.

1Pin down the unitA post, a Reel, a Story? A chat, a message, a conversation? Per day, on a typical day?
2Pick the approachUsually bottom-up from users: people × share active × how often each acts.
3Draw the treePopulation → online → on the app → active that day → split by behaviour → actions.
4Round numbers, one reason eachSplit by what people do (watchers, sometimes-posters, creators), not by age alone.
5Check it another wayActions per user per day, and India’s share of a global figure the company has published.
6Give a range, and what moves itUsually the share of heavy users or the rate for the biggest group.
Key idea · India’s slice of a global number

Companies often publish one global number (WhatsApp: 100 billion+ messages a day; Instagram: 2 billion daily users). India’s share is roughly its share of users, adjusted for how intensely Indians use the app. If India has 18% of the users and uses the app about as much as everyone else, expect about 18% of the activity, not a third. This single check catches most social-media guesstimates that have gone wrong.
