Instagram: a video feed that keeps friends in the loop

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Riya, 22, from Pune, films 30 seconds of a vada pav stall at FC Road, adds a trending song in Instagram’s Edits app and posts it as a Reel. Within seconds Instagram’s servers make four copies at different sizes and check the audio and pictures against its rules. The Reel is first shown to a few hundred people: some of her followers and some strangers who like food videos. They watch it to the end and send it to friends, so Instagram shows it to more. By the next evening it has 40,000 views, 3,000 of them from people who didn’t follow her, 120 shares in DMs and 200 new followers ({{REF:ig-flow}}). Riya earns nothing from Instagram for those views. A café that saw the Reel messages her about a paid post.

Core loop: why people come back

Instagram’s loop has four steps ({{REF:ig-loop}}). A trigger brings you in, often a friend’s message with a Reel. You act: open and watch. The reward is uneven, and that is the point: one Reel in five is really funny, so you keep swiping for the next one. Then you invest: you follow, share or post, which gives Instagram more signals and brings your friends back, starting the loop again for them. The same shape, trigger to action to uneven reward to investment, is Nir Eyal’s “Hooked” model, and it also explains why regulators worry about teenagers’ screen time.

Business model, with numbers

Instagram earns from ads in the feed, Stories and Reels, sold through Meta’s auction. Meta doesn’t report Instagram’s revenue separately, but Instagram is its biggest growth engine: 2 bn daily users (July 2026), and in April–June 2026 Meta’s ad impressions rose 14% and the average price per ad 12%. Creators earn mostly from brands, directly. Instagram tries to take a share of that money by letting brands turn a creator’s post into an ad.

Moat, in plain words. Your friends and favourite creators are here, and leaving means losing both. Its ranking has learned from billions of swipes, which a new app can’t copy quickly. And for Indian creators, Instagram is where brands look first.

  • India’s default app for creators and brands
  • Friends and entertainment in one place
  • Meta’s ad machine behind it

Weaknesses

  • Feels less personal as strangers’ Reels take over
  • Low revenue per Indian user
  • Teen safety and screen-time criticism
Real-life example · Threads: borrowing a network to skip the cold start

Meta launched Threads, a text app like X, in July 2023. New users could sign in with Instagram and follow everyone they already followed there in one tap, so nobody opened an empty app. 10 crore people signed up in five days. Many then drifted away: a borrowed network gets sign-ups, but only posts worth reading bring people back. Meta added messages, communities and a feed users can steer, and Threads reached 500 mn monthly users in June 2026. The lesson for any new social product: bring the network, then give it a reason to stay.
