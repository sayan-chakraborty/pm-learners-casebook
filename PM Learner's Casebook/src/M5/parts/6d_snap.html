Snapchat: the camera company

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Kabir, 17, in Delhi, has a 200-day “streak” with his best friend Aarav: they have sent each other a Snap every single day. At 10:40 pm he remembers. The app opens straight to the camera, not a feed. He picks a cricket-helmet Lens, which the phone itself draws onto his face in real time, snaps a photo and sends it to Aarav. Snap’s servers hold the photo until Aarav opens it, then delete it. The streak counter moves to 201. Aarav replies with a picture of his maths homework ({{REF:sn-flow}}).

Business model, with numbers

In April–June 2026 Snap earned about $1.6 bn, up 19%. Most of it came from ads: Lens ads, Stories ads and ads in Spotlight, its short-video feed. Its “other revenue”, mostly Snapchat+ subscriptions, grew 85% to $316 mn ({{REF:snap-rev}}). Snap has about 1 bn monthly users and added 10 mn daily users that quarter, most of them outside North America and Europe, with India a big contributor. That is Snap’s puzzle: its growth is where each user earns it least.

Moat, in plain words. Your closest friends and your streaks are here. Snap is private and playful, not about follower counts, which is why teenagers use it to talk and Instagram to show off. It also has one of the best AR engines, with tools that let outside creators build Lenses.

Strengths

  • Close friends, daily habit
  • Leader in AR Lenses
  • Subscriptions growing fast

Weaknesses

  • Low ad revenue per user where it grows
  • Streaks can feel like pressure
  • Instagram copies its best ideas (Stories, 2016)

What I’d build next

1. Indian users are active but earn Snap little → self-serve sponsored Lenses for Diwali, IPL and college fests, priced for local brands at ₹5,000 → India revenue per daily user; Lens plays per user.

2. A lost streak makes teenagers anxious → one free “streak freeze” a month → daily users (guardrail: stress reported in surveys).
