Guesstimate 5.2 · WhatsApp chats a day in India GuesstimateMediumIIM B Sigma PM Casebook 2026–27, p. 136
Try it first. Scroll your WhatsApp chat list. How many different chats, people or groups, did you send at least one message in yesterday? Is that typical? Build India’s daily total from there, for five minutes.
{{CASE:B136}}
What worked · what was missing
  • Asked chats or messages, and one-to-one or groups: the right first questions.
  • Stated the approach before starting and checked it with the interviewer.
  • Kept individual and group chats separate, which people use very differently.
  • “Chat” was never defined. A conversation you sent a message in? Every message? The whole answer depends on it.
  • Too many users. 755 million WhatsApp users is well above the 50–55 crore most estimates give for India in 2026.
  • 40% under 18 is far too high. WhatsApp’s minimum age in India is 13; teenagers are a much smaller share of users than of the population.
  • No check. 11 billion conversations a day means 60–70 billion messages, about two-thirds of everything WhatsApp carries worldwide, from a country with under a fifth of its users.

Guesstimate kit check: 1 Unit ✗ asked, but never defined · 2 Approach ✓ · 3 Tree ✓ · 4 Numbers with reasons ✗ · 5 Check ✗ · 6 Range ✗. Skipping card 5 cost the most: WhatsApp’s published 100 billion messages a day would have shown the answer was about four times too high.

Check · “40% of WhatsApp users are under 18” Doesn’t hold up

About a quarter of India’s population is under 15, and WhatsApp requires users in India to be at least 13. Many younger teenagers use a parent’s phone rather than their own account. Under-18s are probably closer to 10% of accounts than 40%, and they happen to be the group the answer gives the most chats.

Stronger answer
A
1 · Unit

I’ll count a “chat” as one conversation, with one person or one group, in which a user sends at least one message that day. Ten messages to your mother count as one chat.

Why this move: “chats” and “messages” differ by about 5–8 times. Defining it out loud stops the interviewer and you from comparing different things.

A
2 · Approach

Bottom-up: WhatsApp users in India → those who send something that day → conversations each. Then check against WhatsApp’s published message total.

Why this move: starting from WhatsApp users (not the whole population) removes two uncertain steps, smartphone share and app share, at once.

A
3–4 · Tree and numbers

About 55 crore WhatsApp users in India. 80% open it on a given day: 44 crore. Of those, 75% send at least one message: 33 crore senders. A sender talks in about 5 one-to-one chats and 3 groups a day: 8 chats. 33 crore × 8 ≈ 264 crore, about 2.6 billion chats a day ({{REF:gtree52}}).

Why this move: “opens” and “sends” are separate steps because many people only read the family group. Mixing them inflates the count.

A
5 · Check

Messages: at about 6 messages per chat, 2.6 billion × 6 ≈ 16 billion messages a day. WhatsApp has said it carries over 100 billion messages a day worldwide, and India is about 18% of its users: 18% × 100 billion = 18 billion. Close. The casebook’s 11 billion chats would be 60–70 billion messages, two-thirds of the world’s traffic.

Why this move: a company’s own published number is the strongest anchor you have. Use it every time one exists.

A
6 · Range

About 2 to 4 billion chats a day. The swing factor is chats per sender: at 6 the total is 2 billion, at 12 it is 4 billion. On Diwali or New Year’s Eve it might double for a day.

Why this move: the range comes with its cause, and the festival note shows you know a typical day differs from a peak.

How the answer hangs togetherDefining a chat as a conversation fixed the unit. Starting from WhatsApp users, then daily openers, then senders, avoided counting readers as writers. WhatsApp’s own 100 billion messages a day, scaled to India’s 18% of users, confirmed about 2.6 billion chats. The range names chats per sender as the input to argue about.
The reusable trick

Define the unit so it can’t be misread, then check against the company’s own number scaled to India’s share of users. For messaging, remember the chain: users → openers → senders → conversations → messages. Each arrow can shrink or grow the number several times.
