Flipkart: India’s biggest sale, and the fight for every day in between

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Ramesh, 38, runs a hardware shop in Indore. His phone is four years old. At midnight on the first day of Big Billion Days, the ₹22,999 phone he has watched for weeks becomes affordable: the app values his old phone at ₹4,000, his bank card takes ₹2,000 off if he pays in six monthly instalments (EMI), and he pays ₹16,999 spread over six months. Key idea · Why the sale is not enough

A phone is bought every two or three years. A shopper who comes only for the October sale is worth one big order and then disappears to Blinkit for chargers, to Meesho for sarees and to Amazon for everything else. Flipkart’s bets since 2024 (Minutes, Shopsy, super.money, zero commission under ₹1,000) all aim at the same thing: getting the sale-day customer to come back in March.

Business model, with numbers. Flipkart earns from seller fees, logistics fees (Ekart), ads and its fintech partners. Its marketplace company reported FY25 revenue of ₹20,493 Cr (+14%) and a loss of ₹1,494 Cr. In November 2025 it dropped commission on goods under ₹1,000; it said sellers’ costs in that range would fall by about 30%. It has moved its holding company back to India, a step before a stock-market listing that has been pushed back more than once. Walmart has owned most of it since 2018.

North StarBuyers who order in at least three different months a year.
InputsPurchases outside sale weeks · share of orders from non-phone categories · Minutes orders · EMI and Pay Later users.
GuardrailsReturn and RTO rate · seller satisfaction · site uptime on sale night.

Moat, in plain words. Phones and large appliances: exclusive launches, the exchange network and bank tie-ups are hard to copy. Ekart reaches towns rivals find costly to serve. And Walmart’s money lets it fight Meesho on price and Blinkit on speed at the same time. The risk is that it fights on too many fronts.

Strengths

  • The biggest sale event; strongest in phones
  • Reach in Tier-2 and Tier-3 India
  • Myntra, a profitable sister company

Weaknesses

  • Still loss-making; listing delayed
  • Weak everyday frequency
  • Squeezed by Meesho on price and quick commerce on speed

What I’d build next

1. Phone buyers vanish for two years → a phone care plan: protection, repair pickup and a guaranteed buy-back value, with an upgrade reminder → repeat phone purchases; care-plan attach rate.

2. Sale-day buyers don’t know Minutes exists → a free first Minutes order (charger, case, screen guard) inside the phone’s delivery message → share of sale-day buyers who place a second order within 30 days.
