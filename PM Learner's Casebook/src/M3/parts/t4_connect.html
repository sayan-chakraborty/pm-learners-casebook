Back to Jaipur · A sketch answer for the kurta question

Is a 30% return rate on kurtas worth solving? Size it first. At about ₹250 lost per return and ₹195 earned per kept order, 10 kurtas at 30% returns earn ₹615; at 20%, ₹1,060. Ten points of return rate is worth about 70% more money per order, so yes, if the rate can move.

For whom? The platform (logistics cost), the seller (unsold stock that comes back creased) and the customer (a week of waiting and a trip to hand it back). All three gain, so it is a rare problem with no losing side.

How would you know? Split the rate: is it high because of the product (one brand’s sizing), the customer (bracketing two sizes), or the season (wedding-wear mix)? Read the reason codes. Then act before the order, where a misfit costs ₹0: a fit hint from each product’s returns, and size advice from the customer’s own kept orders.

Goal (North Star): kept kurtas per 100 visitors. Safety checks (guardrails): conversion rate; complaints about refused returns. Trade-off: blocking returns lowers the rate and the sales together; only fixes that help people choose right lower one without the other.

Key idea · The e-commerce PM’s three questions
  1. Does this change the number of orders that are kept, or only the number placed?
  2. Who pays for it: the customer, the seller, a brand’s ad budget or the platform?
  3. Which side of the marketplace does it help, and does the other side lose?
In the interview

In any shopping case, put a rupee number on one order within the first three minutes: “a ₹1,299 kurta leaves the app about ₹195 if kept and costs about ₹250 if returned”. It shows you understand how the business earns before you change it.
