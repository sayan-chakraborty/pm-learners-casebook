Guesstimate 3.1 · Google searches per second GuesstimateMediumIIM B Sigma PM Casebook 2026–27, p. 141
Try it first. How many times did you search on Google yesterday, including voice and Lens? Is that more or less than a typical internet user worldwide? Build from there for five minutes.
{{CASE:B141}}
What worked · what was missing
  • Clarified geography and device, then stated the approach before any number.
  • Removed China, where Google is blocked: a sharp, specific step.
  • Split users by how much they search (power, casual, occasional) instead of one average.
  • Adjusted for AI chat tools when prompted, and redid the maths.
  • Never checked against a known figure. Google said in 2025 that it handles “more than 5 trillion searches a year”, about 158,000 a second.
  • No per-person sanity check. 19 billion ÷ 3.6 billion users is 5.3 searches a day each. Plausible, but it should be said out loud.
  • Average or peak? For servers, the peak hour matters, roughly 1.5–2× the average.
  • Undercounted people online: 60% of the non-China world, when ITU puts worldwide use at 74% in 2025.

Check · “Internet penetration globally is ~60%” Directionally right

ITU estimates about 6 billion people online in 2025, 74% of the world. Take away about 1.1 billion online in China and roughly 4.9 billion remain, against the candidate’s 4 billion. The error happened to cancel out a high searches-per-person figure, so the answer landed close. Two errors that cancel are luck, not method.

Stronger answer
A
1 · Unit

I’ll count every query Google answers, typed, spoken or through Lens, worldwide, averaged over a typical day. Then I’ll say what the busiest hour looks like, because servers are built for the peak.

Why this move: “per second” can mean average or peak; saying which avoids a mismatch with what the interviewer had in mind.

A
2 · Approach

Top-down, from the demand side: the number of people who use Google, × searches each person makes a day, ÷ the seconds in a day.

Why this move: people and habits are easier to estimate than Google’s servers, and a published figure exists to check against.

A
3–4 · Tree and numbers

About 6 billion people are online (ITU, 2025). About 1.1 billion of them are in China, where Google is blocked: 6 − 1.1 = 4.9 billion. Google has about 90% of search: 4.9 × 90% ≈ 4.4 billion searchers. On average each searches about 3 times a day: heavy users do 10 or more, many do one or none. 4.4 billion × 3 = 13 billion searches a day. 13 billion ÷ 86,400 seconds ≈ 150,000 a second ({{REF:gtree}}).

Why this move: three multiplications, each with a one-line reason, are easy for the interviewer to follow and to challenge.

A
5 · Check

Google said in 2025 that it handles more than 5 trillion searches a year. 5 trillion ÷ 365 ≈ 13.7 billion a day; ÷ 86,400 ≈ 158,000 a second. My 150,000 is within 5% of that.

Why this move: a second route to the answer turns a guess into an estimate you can defend.

A
6 · Range

The number I’m least sure of is searches per person. At 2 a day the answer is about 100,000 a second; at 5 a day, about 250,000. At the busiest hour traffic runs roughly 1.5–2× the average, so Google must be built for about 250,000–300,000 a second. AI chat tools push the per-person number down; AI Overviews and Lens push it up.

Why this move: a range with its main driver shows judgement, and tells the listener what to watch.

How the answer hangs togetherThe unit (average, all query types, worldwide) set up a simple demand-side tree. Each number has a reason, the check against Google’s own figure confirms the scale, and the range names the one input that moves it.
The reusable trick

Per second = per day ÷ 86,400, which is about 1 lakh. So “a billion a day” is about 11,500 a second, and “10 crore a day” (UPI is near 79 crore) is about 1,150 a second. Remember one pair and you can convert any daily number to a per-second rate in your head.
