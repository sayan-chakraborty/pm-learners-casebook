Google Search: people say what they want, and advertisers pay to answer

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Karan, 27, in Hyderabad, types “buy running shoes online” into Chrome. Within a quarter of a second he sees an AI Overview summarising what to look for, two ads above the ordinary results, and a row of shopping listings. He clicks the second ad, from a shoe shop, and lands on its page. In that quarter-second an auction ran among every advertiser bidding on his words, and the shop agreed to pay Google only because he clicked ({{REF:gs-walk}}).

How the search ad auction works

Google doesn’t simply sell the top slot to the highest bidder. Each ad gets a score: Ad Rank ≈ bid × quality, where quality is Google’s estimate of how likely people are to click and how useful the landing page is. The winner pays only enough to keep its place above the next ad. So a relevant ad can win with a lower bid, and pay less ({{REF:gs-auc}}). Everyone gains: users see more useful ads, good advertisers pay less, and Google earns more clicks overall.

Business model, with numbers. In April–June 2026 Alphabet earned $119.8 bn (+24%). Search and other: $63.3 bn (+17%), still growing in the age of chatbots. YouTube ads: $11.1 bn; Google Cloud: $24.8 bn. Google said in 2025 it handles over 5 trillion searches a year, about 1.25 trillion a quarter. So very roughly, $63 bn ÷ 1.25 trillion ≈ 5 cents of revenue per search, most of it from the small share of searches with buying intent.

Moat, in plain words. Being the default: the search box on Android, in Chrome, and (through payments to Apple) in Safari. Twenty-five years of click data that tell it which result satisfies which question. And advertisers who already run campaigns there. In August 2024 a US court found Google had illegally kept its search monopoly; in September 2025 it ordered remedies that stopped short of selling Chrome but barred exclusive default deals.

Strengths

  • Default search almost everywhere
  • The highest-intent ad inventory in the world
  • Its own AI models, chips and data centres

Weaknesses

  • AI answers can cut clicks to the websites that feed it
  • Chatbots compete for the “answer me” queries
  • Antitrust limits on default deals

What I’d build next

1. AI answers satisfy users but starve the websites that wrote the content → clearer source cards with a one-tap “read more” inside AI answers → clicks to publishers per AI answer, with satisfied searches held steady.

2. Small Indian shops can’t write good ads → AI-made ads in Hindi and regional languages from a shop’s Google Business Profile → new small advertisers; their spend after 90 days.
