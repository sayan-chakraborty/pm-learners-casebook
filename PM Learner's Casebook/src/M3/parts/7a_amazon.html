Amazon: the store that is really a delivery network and an ad business

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Neha, 31, a Prime member in Bengaluru, needs running shoes for flat feet. She types the question into the Amazon app’s AI assistant (launched as Rufus, now merged with Alexa+ into one shopping assistant). It reads reviews and product specs and suggests three pairs; the first result is a sponsored listing. She buys a ₹3,499 pair with free next-day delivery because she’s a Prime member. The shoes come from a fulfilment centre (FC: Amazon’s warehouse, where sellers store stock) 18 km away. She never thinks about the seller, who is paid about a week later ({{REF:am-walk}}).

Core loop: the flywheel

Jeff Bezos sketched Amazon’s strategy on a napkin in 2001 as a loop with no start and no end ({{REF:am-loop}}). More selection gives shoppers a better experience; more shoppers attract more sellers; more orders spread the cost of warehouses and trucks, which allows lower prices, which brings more shoppers. Prime adds a habit to the loop: once you have paid the fee, you shop at Amazon first. And in the last decade ads were added on top: more shoppers make every sponsored slot more valuable, and that ad money pays for faster delivery.

Business model, with numbers

Amazon doesn’t report India on its own. Worldwide, in April–June 2026, it had revenue of $200.6 bn (+20%). Two lines matter most for understanding its shop: advertising, $19.8 bn (+26%), almost all profit, and AWS, $42.2 bn (+37%), its cloud business, which earns most of the company’s operating profit. In India, the store earns from seller fees (referral, fulfilment, closing fees), ads, Prime fees and payments. From 16 March 2026 it removed referral fees on items under ₹1,000 across 1,800+ categories, matching Flipkart and Meesho; sellers now pay mainly through fulfilment fees and ads.

Moat, in plain words. The delivery network: a nationwide network of fulfilment centres, sort centres and delivery stations took a decade and billions of dollars to build, and they let Amazon promise next-day delivery. The Prime habit: members check Amazon first. And the ad data: Amazon knows what people actually buy, not just what they look at, which makes its ads worth more to brands.

Strengths

  • Fastest reliable delivery; the Prime habit
  • Ads and AWS pay for price cuts and speed
  • Trusted for electronics and branded goods

Weaknesses

  • Weaker than Flipkart in smaller towns and in the big October sale
  • Too many ads on the first screen can hurt conversion
  • FDI rules limit how much it can control stock and prices

What I’d build next

1. Tier-2 shoppers don’t trust fit or quality for clothes → “Kept by people like you”: show the share of buyers in your size who kept the item, next to the price → return rate on fashion; conversion.

2. The AI assistant answers questions but can’t follow up after delivery → the assistant asks “did they fit?” three days later and offers an exchange in one tap → exchanges instead of refunds; repeat purchases.

3. Prime members in small towns see little value in video → promote the ₹399 Shopping Edition to them, with local-language delivery tracking → paid members outside metros; orders per member.
