Groww vs Zerodha vs smallcase: three ways to earn from investors

Walkthrough (illustrative personas). Priya, 23, gets her first salary in Pune and wants to “start investing”. On Groww she finishes KYC in minutes: a PAN check, her address pulled through DigiLocker, and a ₹1 test deposit to her bank account. She picks a Nifty 50 index fund and sets a ₹2,000 SIP (a fixed monthly investment) on the 5th, paid by UPI Autopay. Karan, 35, is the opposite user: he trades options on Zerodha’s Kite on a laptop, many times a day. A year later Priya buys a themed “basket” of 12 stocks through smallcase, inside her broker’s app, for a ₹100 fee. Three companies, three very different ways of making money from the same investors.

Real-life example · What SEBI’s F&O rules did to both brokers

In September 2024 SEBI found that 93% of individual futures-and-options traders lost money over FY22–FY24. From late 2024 it made contracts larger, limited weekly expiries, and made exchanges charge all brokers the same fees, which ended the rebates brokers used to earn. Zerodha, built on active traders, felt it most: FY26 brokerage income fell about 11% and its exchange-rebate income went to zero, though profit held at about ₹4,300 Cr thanks to margin-trading interest. Groww, with a broader base of small investors, kept growing: Q1 FY27 revenue was up 66% and profit ₹735 Cr.

2025–26 moves. Groww listed on the stock exchanges (Nov 2025) and posted a profit of ₹2,083 Cr in its first year as a listed company. Zerodha’s FY26 was flat, with margin-trading interest now about a tenth of revenue. smallcase crossed ₹100 Cr of revenue in FY25 and narrowed its losses.

Strengths

  • Groww: the easiest first step for millions of new investors
  • Zerodha: the most profit per client, with no outside funding
  • smallcase: distribution through other brokers’ apps, at almost no acquisition cost

Weaknesses

  • Groww: earns most on the riskiest products it sells to beginners
  • Zerodha: revenue tied to trading intensity, which regulators are cooling
  • smallcase: small revenue; depends on brokers keeping it embedded

What I’d build next

1. Groww. Many first SIPs stop within a year, often after the first market fall → a “stay the course” screen after a dip that shows her own SIP’s long-run average cost → SIPs still live at 12 months.

2. Zerodha. Most F&O traders lose money, and losses often come in streaks → an optional cooling-off: after three losing weeks, suggest a one-week pause on option buying, with the trader’s own numbers → share of traders who take the pause, and their 6-month outcome against similar traders who don’t.

3. smallcase. Investors buy a basket once and forget it → basket SIPs tied to a named goal (“Home deposit, 2030”) with progress shown in the broker’s app → basket-SIP continuation at 12 months.
