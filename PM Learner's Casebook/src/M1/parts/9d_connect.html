Back to Surat · A sketch answer for Hitesh

What changes on 15 October. On ₹25 lakh a month of payments above ₹2,000, Hitesh now pays about ₹10,000 a month. His cheapest escape is asking buyers to pay his personal UPI ID, which is free. That hurts PhonePe twice: its share of the new fee disappears, and business payments become harder to see, which weakens the payment history it would use to lend to him.

Scope first. Goal: keep large merchants’ business payments on their business QR. Who: high-ticket merchants taking ₹1 lakh+ a month. Constraint: the fee is set by NPCI; PhonePe can’t waive it.

What I’d build. (1) A plain fee statement: the fee per payment, per day and per month, so nobody is surprised. (2) Reasons to stay that are worth more than 0.4%: next-morning settlement, GST-ready invoices for every payment, and working-capital credit priced on his business QR history. (3) A soundbox message that confirms business payments by the buyer’s firm name, which a personal-UPI transfer can’t.

Goal (North Star): share of large merchants’ payment value still on business QRs 60 days after 15 October. Safety check (guardrail): large merchants switching to a rival app, and support complaints about fees. Trade-off: credit offers bring in revenue but carry the risk of defaults, so start with merchants who have 12 months of history.

Key idea · The payments PM’s three questions
  1. Who pays for this, and who earns?
  2. What happens if money is stuck halfway?
  3. How will we know our change caused the result?
In the interview

In any payments case, ask question 1 within the first two minutes. It is the fastest way to show you understand the industry, and it shapes every metric that follows.
