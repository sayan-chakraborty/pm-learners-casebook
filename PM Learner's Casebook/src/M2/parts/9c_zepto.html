Key idea · Why fast growth looks like big losses

Imagine stores that each lose ₹3 lakh a month in their first six months and make ₹1 lakh a month after that. A company that opens 50 new stores this quarter reports a bigger loss than last quarter, even though every old store is improving. So ask two questions of any quick-commerce loss: what share of stores are under six months old, and what do the mature stores earn? Blinkit’s first profit came when its expansion slowed and most stores had matured.

Business model and 2026 reality. Zepto earns the margin on goods sold through its stores, customer fees, café margins and brand ads. Press reports put FY26 revenue at about ₹22,600 Cr and the loss at about ₹5,900 Cr (not yet checked against filings). It moved its holding company from Singapore to India in 2025, filed for an IPO, and in July 2026 put the IPO on hold and raised money privately instead.

Moat, in plain words. Thin. Speed is copyable by anyone with stores in the same streets, and Blinkit, Instamart, Flipkart Minutes and Amazon Now all have them. What Zepto had was execution speed and a young brand; what it lacks is a profitable second business to pay for the fight.

Strengths

  • Fastest-growing challenger; strong with young urban users
  • Café and ads raise margin per basket

Weaknesses

  • Large losses; IPO on hold
  • No profitable core business, unlike Eternal or Swiggy

What I’d build next

1. Young stores lose money until orders build → open new stores only where a nearby store already fills 1,000 orders a day, and move its far orders to the new store → months to breakeven per new store.

2. Brands can’t see which stores sell their products → self-serve store-level sales and ad results for brands → ad revenue per brand; brands advertising again next quarter.
