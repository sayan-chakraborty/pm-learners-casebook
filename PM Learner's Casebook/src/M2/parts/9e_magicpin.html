magicpin and ONDC: food delivery on an open network

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Sameer, 27, in Delhi, noticed that his usual paneer roll costs less on magicpin than on the big apps: same shop, same roll. He searches, orders for ₹240, and a rider from a separate delivery company brings it. What he doesn’t see is that four different companies handled his order, all speaking one open protocol run by ONDC, the government-backed Open Network for Digital Commerce ({{REF:ondc-walk}}).

Check · “ONDC will break the food-delivery duopoly” Overstated

At about 1.5 lakh orders a day in late 2024, magicpin’s ONDC food business was a few per cent of the big two’s volume. Lower fees help restaurants, but customers choose on range, reliability and habit, and those still sit with Zomato and Swiggy. A better claim: ONDC puts a ceiling on how far commissions can rise, because restaurants now have somewhere else to go.

Business model and scale. magicpin earns fees as a buyer app, delivery fees and brand deals. It became the largest food buyer app on ONDC, at about 1.5 lakh orders a day (Oct 2024). ONDC’s own incentives, which once paid part of each order, have been cut back, and that tests how many customers stay without them.

North StarWeekly repeat orders without any subsidy.

Moat, in plain words. Very little of its own: any buyer app can plug into the same restaurants on ONDC. Its edge is being early, a loyalty base and a delivery fleet. The deeper question is whether an open network can match the reliability of one company that controls the whole chain: when an ONDC order fails, whose fault is it, and who refunds?

Strengths

  • Real price advantage for the same food
  • Restaurants actively want an alternative

Weaknesses

  • Smaller range and less reliable delivery than the big two
  • Growth leaned on ONDC incentives

What I’d build next

1. Users can’t tell who is responsible when an order goes wrong → a single magicpin guarantee: refund in 10 minutes, whichever company failed → repeat rate after a failed order.

2. The price gap is the reason to switch, but users can’t see it → show “₹38 less than on other apps” on each dish, from restaurants’ own price data → conversion on dishes showing the gap.
