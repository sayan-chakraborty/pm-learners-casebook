Back to Mumbai · A sketch answer for the monsoon evening

Scope first. Goal: deliver as many orders as possible on time tonight without putting riders at risk. Who: customers, riders and restaurants in the flooded zones. Constraint: rider safety is not up for trade.

Next 30 minutes. (1) Make the promise honest before payment: wider arrival ranges calculated from live rider supply. (2) A rain fee shown up front on every order, funding a per-order rain incentive for riders who choose to stay online. (3) Hide restaurants more than about 4 km away and pause zones with waterlogged roads. (4) Batch orders from the same restaurant cluster so each ride carries two. (5) On the tech side, give the rider-dispatch queue priority over invoices and analytics.

What must not break. No incentive that pays riders to ride through water; no fee added after payment; no promise the fleet can’t keep.

Goal (North Star): orders delivered on time per hour in the affected zones. Safety checks (guardrails): customer cancellations, rider incident reports, complaints about fees. Trade-off: fewer orders taken tonight, in exchange for promises kept and riders safe.

Key idea · The delivery PM’s three questions
  1. Where do the minutes go, and which step can we change?
  2. What does this order earn after the rider, the discount and the refund?
  3. Which side (customer, restaurant, rider, brand) pays for this change?
In the interview

In any delivery or quick-commerce case, break the time or the money into steps within the first three minutes: “A 12-minute order is assignment, picking, the ride and the gate”, or “a ₹450 order earns about ₹145 and pays ₹85 to the rider”. It shows you know how the machine works before you try to fix it.
