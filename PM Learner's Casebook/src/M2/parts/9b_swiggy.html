Swiggy and Instamart: one member, one fleet, many services

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Neha, 34, a consultant in Pune, pays for Swiggy One. At 8 pm she orders dal makhani and rotis for ₹380; the membership makes delivery free. While waiting she remembers breakfast, taps the Instamart tab in the same app, and adds eggs and bread for ₹240. Dinner arrives at 8:34, groceries at 8:47. It feels like one shop. Behind it, two businesses share one customer, one wallet and often one rider ({{REF:sw-walk}}).

Moat, in plain words. A shared fleet and a shared membership: every service Swiggy adds makes both cheaper per order. It is weaker than it sounds, because Zomato and Blinkit together match it in most big cities and Blinkit has more stores.

2025–26 moves. Instamart chose profit over size: it dropped millions of unprofitable, small-basket users and pushed bigger baskets, reaching contribution breakeven in May 2026. Bolt grew as a share of food orders. Snacc, a separate 10-minute snacks app, was tried in 2025. Advertising spend stayed high (about ₹1,160 Cr in Apr–Jun 2026) to hold share.

Strengths

  • Highest basket size in quick commerce
  • One app, one fleet, one membership
  • Food business profitable and growing

Weaknesses

  • Number two in food, number three in quick commerce
  • Still loss-making as a group
  • Fewer dark stores than Blinkit

What I’d build next

1. Members don’t notice Instamart while waiting for food → an “add groceries, same rider” card on the order-tracking screen for pods under 1 km away → share of food orders with a grocery add-on.

2. Bolt users can’t tell what is fresh-cooked → show “made to order” vs “ready-made” on each Bolt dish → Bolt repeat rate and ratings.
