What worked · what was missing
  • Confirmed the product in one line and asked for the goal before listing a single metric.
  • Wrote the goal as a formula: order value = orders × AOV, and orders broken down further. That is a driver tree.
  • Scoped the platform (app only) with one question.
  • Walked the journey first, so the metrics follow what the user does.
  • About 40 metrics, none chosen. No North Star, no guardrail, no target.
  • The buckets are AARRR without Revenue, although order value was the stated goal.
  • The formula forgets frequency, the biggest lever in grocery (see Check).
  • BB Now (10–20 minutes) and scheduled slots were never split, though their baskets differ a lot.

Scoping kit check: Confirm the thing ✓ · Goal ✓ · Who: new or repeat buyers? ✗ skipped · Today and the trigger ✗ skipped · Edges ✓ (app only). Metrics add-on, “what decision will these metrics drive?”: ✗ skipped, which is how a list of 40 happens.

Check · “Orders = visitors × add-to-cart rate × purchase conversion” Directionally right

True for one visit. But groceries are a habit: a household that orders every week and four strangers who order once look the same in this formula. For a month, a better split is orders = monthly buyers × orders per buyer. It shows at once whether growth is coming from new households or from existing ones ordering more often.

Check · “Daily active users as an engagement metric” Overstated

Families buy groceries three to six times a month. A daily-use ratio would call a healthy weekly habit “weak engagement” and push the team towards notifications nobody wants. Match the metric to the natural rhythm of the job: here, orders per buyer per month.

Stronger answer
A
① Clarify

Over what time frame, and should I include both BB Now and scheduled delivery? I’ll assume the goal is order value from the app over the next two quarters, from both new and existing buyers.

Q

Both services, two quarters. Go ahead.

A
② Formula

Monthly app order value = monthly buyers × orders per buyer × AOV. Illustrative: 40 lakh × 3.5 × ₹750 = ₹1,050 Cr a month. Lifting any one factor by 10% adds ₹105 Cr. For households that already buy, frequency is the cheapest to move.

Why this move: the formula shows which lever is worth the most before any metric is named.

A
③ Stages

I’ll follow the buyer through five stages ({{REF:aarrr}}): they find the app, place a first order, come back, spend more, and tell others.

A
④ One each

Acquisition: cost per first order. Activation: share of new installers who order within 7 days. Retention: share of first-time buyers who order again within 30 days. Revenue: orders per buyer per month, and AOV. Referral: invites accepted per 100 buyers.

A
⑤ North Star

Goal (North Star): monthly order value from repeat buyers (households with two or more orders in the month). It grows only if people come back and buy more, which is what the business goal needs.

A
⑥ Guardrails

Safety checks (guardrails): on-time delivery %; missing or substituted items per 100 orders; contribution margin per order, so discounts don’t buy the growth. Trade-off: pushing BB Now raises frequency but shrinks baskets, so I’d watch the mix. Next: a weekly review of the five stage metrics, split by service.

Framework · AARRR, the customer funnel

1 · The situation.The candidate grouped 40 metrics into buckets. The buckets were almost right; what was missing was one number per bucket and the reason for it.

2 · An everyday example.A new gym opens in your colony. Some people see the poster. A few come for a free trial. Some enjoy the first workout. Fewer are still coming in week six. Those pay for a six-month plan. A couple bring a friend. If the gym is failing, it matters a lot which of those steps is leaking.

3 · The framework.AARRR (Dave McClure’s “pirate metrics”, 2007) splits a customer’s life into five stages: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral. Measure the conversion from each stage to the next, find the biggest leak, and work there. Activation means the first moment the user gets the promised value, here a first order that arrives complete.

4 · When it misleads.The funnel invites a list of metrics per stage, which is how this candidate reached 40. It also draws acquisition at the top, as if it mattered most. A gym that spends on posters while members stop coming in week two is filling a leaking bucket. In grocery the order of attention is often retention, activation, revenue, and acquisition last.

5 · In the interview.“I’ll walk the five stages and pick one metric for each. Then I’ll choose the North Star from the stage that moves order value most, which for groceries is repeat buying.”
