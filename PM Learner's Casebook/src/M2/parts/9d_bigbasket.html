BigBasket: the planned weekly shop in a ten-minute world

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Mrs Iyer, 58, in Chennai, orders the week’s groceries every Sunday night: vegetables, rice, dal, oil and cleaning supplies, ₹2,150 in all, for the 7–9 am slot. She trusts BigBasket’s vegetables more than the corner shop’s and earns NeuCoins she spends on Tata products. On Wednesday she runs out of curd and uses BB Now, which arrives in 18 minutes. One app, two machines ({{REF:bb-walk}}).

Real-life example · Why a van beats a scooter, sometimes

Illustrative: a van costs ₹2,400 for a morning route of 20 homes, so ₹120 an order on baskets of ₹2,000+ (6% of the basket). A 10-minute rider costs ₹45 on a ₹500 basket (9%). Per rupee of groceries the van is cheaper; per order the scooter is. That is why BigBasket keeps both: slots for the weekly shop, BB Now for top-ups. The risk is that the top-ups slowly replace the weekly shop, and the cheaper machine goes idle.

Moat, in plain words. Fresh-produce sourcing and grading, built over a decade, and the trust of planned, older, family shoppers. The weakness: it came late to speed, grows slowly (6%), and its app is opened weekly while rivals’ are opened daily.

Strengths

  • Best in fresh produce and big planned baskets
  • Tata’s balance sheet and loyalty network

Weaknesses

  • Slow growth, widening loss
  • Seen as slower and less exciting by young users

What I’d build next

1. Weekly shoppers forget items and turn to rivals mid-week → a “top up before Wednesday” reminder built from each household’s usual run-out day, delivered by BB Now → share of weekly buyers with a mid-week BB Now order.

2. Produce quality is BigBasket’s edge but invisible in a 10-minute app → a “picked today, graded A” tag with the farm region → fresh-category conversion and complaints.
