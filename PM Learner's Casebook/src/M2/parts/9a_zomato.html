Zomato and Blinkit: profit from food, growth from ten minutes

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). 8:40 pm. Kavya opens Zomato. The top row says “Order again” and shows the biryani she had two Fridays ago. She taps it, sees “arrives in 28–33 min”, and the bill: ₹450 food, ₹25 delivery, ₹15 platform fee. She pays by UPI. A map shows the restaurant preparing, then a rider’s dot moving towards her. At the door she reads out a four-digit PIN. The whole visible journey takes four taps; {{REF:zm-walk}} shows the relay behind them.

The second app. 10:52 pm, Gurugram. Rohan, a student, realises there is no milk for the morning and his laptop charger has died. On Blinkit he adds both, pays ₹1,340, and a rider is at his door at 11:03. The store 1.8 km away never saw him; its picker saw only a list of shelf locations ({{REF:bk-walk}}).

Business model in one line. Food earns a commission, customer fees and ads on each order, and turns about 5–6% of order value into profit. Blinkit now owns much of its stock, so its revenue counts the full price of goods (which is why Eternal’s revenue jumped 182% to ₹20,211 Cr), and it earns the product margin plus brand ads. Hyperpure sells supplies to restaurants.

North StarOrders delivered per week (food) and order value per store per day (Blinkit).
InputsMonthly transacting customers · orders per customer · top restaurants online at 8 pm · store fill rate · share of mature stores.
GuardrailsCancellations · on-time % · rider earnings per hour · inventory written off as % of sales.

Moat, in plain words. Density. Zomato has more restaurants and more orders in most neighbourhoods, so its riders are busier and its delivery cheaper. Blinkit has the most dark stores (2,443 in Jun 2026), and a mature store handles about ₹8.3 lakh of orders a day, which a newcomer’s half-empty store can’t match. Both cracks are the same: restaurants and brands list on every app, and customers switch for a ₹50 coupon.

2025–26 moves. Renamed the parent Eternal (2025). Blinkit moved to owning stock after Eternal became Indian-owned and controlled. District, a separate app for dining out, films and events. Bistro, a 10-minute café app. Zomato’s own 10-minute food experiments shut. Blinkit posted its first adjusted profit (Apr–Jun 2026).

Strengths

  • Leader in food and in quick commerce, both profitable at the operating level
  • Dense store network and a store economics playbook rivals copy
  • Food profits fund experiments without raising money

Weaknesses

  • Food growth has slowed to about 20% a year
  • Owning stock adds waste and working capital risk
  • Restaurant goodwill: the CCI report and commission complaints

What I’d build next

1. Families order dinner separately on four phones → a shared family cart with one payer and one delivery → AOV and orders per household.

2. On rain nights promised times break and cancellations spike → a “rain mode” that widens the time range before payment and hides restaurants beyond 5 km → cancellations on rain nights vs dry nights.

3. Blinkit stores waste fresh stock that is close to expiry → a 7–10 pm “use today” shelf at a discount, shown only near the store → inventory written off as % of sales.
