Guesstimate 7.2 · Google Meet calls a day GuesstimateEasyIIM B Sigma PM Casebook 2026–27, p. 143
Try it first. Count the Google Meet calls you joined last week, and how many people were in each. Then estimate how many Meet calls happen in the world on a weekday, for five minutes.
{{CASE:B143}}
What worked · what was missing
  • Asked scope first (region, work or personal) and gave a reason for choosing work calls.
  • Laid out four steps and got agreement before calculating.
  • Clean arithmetic, easy to follow and easy to correct.
  • Counted joins, not meetings. If four colleagues each join a call, the answer counts four meetings. It needs dividing by people per meeting.
  • 84 crore professional users is almost three times the “over 30 crore monthly users” Google has reported for Meet in total.
  • Ten meetings a week for every user is a manager’s calendar; many monthly users join once or twice a week, and schools and personal calls use Meet too.
  • No check and no range.

Guesstimate kit check: 1 Unit ✗ joins vs meetings · 2 Approach ✓ · 3 Tree ✓ · 4 Numbers with reasons ✗ 25% and 10 a week unexplained · 5 Check ✗ · 6 Range ✗. Card 1 again: the right number of people, multiplied in the wrong unit, overcounts by the size of a meeting.

Check · “1.68 billion” Google Meets a day Doesn’t hold up

In April 2020, at the height of lockdowns, Google said Meet had more than 10 crore daily meeting participants. The casebook’s 168 crore meetings would need, at three or four people each, 500 to 700 crore joins a day: more than 50 times the lockdown peak, and more joins than there are internet users. The daily number of meetings is more likely in the single-digit crores.

Stronger answer
A
1 · Unit

Separate Meet calls held worldwide on a typical weekday, for work, school and personal use. A call with five people counts once.

Why this move: saying “counts once” at the start forces the division by meeting size later, which is the whole difference between the two answers.

A
2 · Approach

Start from Meet’s users rather than the world’s population, since Google has published a user figure: over 30 crore monthly users.

Why this move: when a company number exists, starting from it removes three shaky steps (internet share, Google share, professional share).

A
3–4 · Tree and numbers

30 crore monthly users × 35% who use it on a given weekday (heavy office users daily, students and occasional users rarely) = 10.5 crore people. × 2 calls joined each = 21 crore joins. ÷ 3.5 people per call (many one-to-one calls, some large ones) = about 6 crore meetings a weekday ({{REF:gtree72}}).

Why this move: each rate is small and defensible, and the last step turns joins into meetings.

A
5 · Check

10.5 crore people joining on a weekday is close to the 10 crore daily participants Google reported in April 2020. Remote work has fallen since, but Meet’s user base has grown, so being close to that figure makes sense.

Why this move: the check is on people, the part of the tree the company published, which tests every step before the division.

A
6 · Range

3–11 crore meetings a weekday, most likely about 6 crore. The swing factor is people per meeting and weekday use: 5 people and 25% daily use gives 3 crore; 2.5 people and 45% gives about 11 crore.

Why this move: meeting size is the number nobody outside Google knows, so the range is honest about it.

How the answer hangs togetherDefining a meeting as counting once forced the division by meeting size. Starting from Google’s own user figure removed the guesswork about populations. The daily-participant check confirms the people part of the tree, and the range is built around the one unknown that remains, how many people a typical call has.
The reusable trick

Meetings = people joining × joins each ÷ people per meeting. Whenever a count involves groups (calls, rides shared, orders split), divide by group size before you answer.
