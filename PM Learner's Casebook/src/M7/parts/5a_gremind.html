Guesstimates: counting calls and meetings

Video calls are how doctors consult, tutors teach and teams meet, and both guesstimates here are about them. Each goes wrong the same way: counting the wrong thing. A meeting of four people is one meeting but four joins. A video that goes up to the server once comes down to three other screens. Before multiplying anything, say exactly what one unit is. The six cards still do the rest.

1Pin down the unitData in, out, or both? A meeting, or a person joining one?
2Pick the approachFrom people who call (demand), or from servers and bandwidth (supply)?
3Draw the treeWorkers → on a call today → this app → hours → data per hour.
4Round numbers, one reason eachWhy 40% on a call on a weekday? Why 3.5 people a meeting?
5Check it another wayAgainst the company’s own published minutes or users.
6Give a range, and what moves itUsually the app’s share, and how many people are in a typical call.
Key idea · Count the right unit

Most guesstimate errors aren’t bad numbers; they are the right numbers multiplied in the wrong unit. “Users × meetings each” counts joins, so divide by people per meeting to get meetings. “Users × data each” counts one direction, so add what the server sends back. Say the unit out loud at card 1 and again before you give the answer.
