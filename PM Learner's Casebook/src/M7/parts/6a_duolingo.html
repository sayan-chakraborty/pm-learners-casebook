Duolingo: a daily habit, three minutes at a time

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Neha, 27, has learned Spanish on Duolingo for 212 days, mostly in the office lift. At 8:50 am her phone shows “Your 212-day streak is waiting”. She taps it, and a lesson of about fifteen exercises opens: match words, type a sentence, listen and pick, speak a line. Most are easy; two past-tense verbs trip her up, and those come back later in the lesson. Three minutes later: “Lesson complete, +15 points”, the streak flame reads 213, and a banner says she has moved up to fourth in this week’s league of 30 learners. She puts the phone away before the lift doors open ({{REF:duo-flow}}).

Core loop: why people come back

The loop is short on purpose ({{REF:duo-loop}}): a lesson takes minutes, the streak and points rise, the league gives a reason to do one more, and a reminder at the learner’s usual time brings them back tomorrow. Each part pushes ability up or supplies a prompt, which is why Duolingo is often used as the textbook case of habit design. The growth loop is the same streak seen from outside: people share milestone streaks, and friends on the app nudge each other. The Q2 2026 letter credited part of the jump in daily users to a one-time “Streak Revival” campaign in June, which brought back people who had lost their streaks.

Business model, with numbers

Freemium: the full course is free with ads and limits, and learners pay to remove them. In April–June 2026 Duolingo had 5.87 crore daily users (up 23%), 1.27 crore paid subscribers (up 17%) and revenue of $298.5 million (up 18%), though bookings, the money actually taken in the quarter, grew only 8%. Most revenue comes from subscriptions: Super (no ads, unlimited mistakes) and Max (adds AI features). Ads on the free tier and the Duolingo English Test, a cheap online exam accepted by many universities, add the rest. The key number is that roughly one in five daily users pays, which is high for a free app.

Moat, in plain words. Hundreds of millions of learners’ answers train the exercise picker and the reminders, so a new rival starts with cruder lessons. The streak is also a switching cost: a learner with 400 days on Duolingo won’t start from zero elsewhere.

2025–26 moves. An “AI-first” memo in April 2025 (AI to create courses, fewer contractors) drew a public backlash on social media, then 148 new AI-built courses; chess and music courses; AI video calls in Max; and the June 2026 Streak Revival.

Strengths

  • The best habit machine in consumer apps
  • High share of users who pay
  • Cheap to add courses with AI

Weaknesses

  • Streaks can drift from real learning
  • Weak at speaking fluency
  • AI tools can copy the lessons

What I’d build next

1. Learners keep streaks alive on the easiest lessons → a weekly “can you still do this?” check that counts toward the streak only when passed → share of streak days with real practice; test scores after 90 days.

2. Indian learners of English want to speak at work → short AI role-plays for interviews and customer calls, in Hindi-to-English and Tamil-to-English courses → speaking minutes per learner; Max upgrades in India.

3. Speaking exercises are skipped in public → serve them when headphones are connected, and save them for later in the day otherwise → speaking exercises done per learner without a fall in lessons finished.
