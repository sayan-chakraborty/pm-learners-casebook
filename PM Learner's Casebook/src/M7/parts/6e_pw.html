PhysicsWallah: free on YouTube, paid in the classroom

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Aman, 16, in Patna, wants to crack JEE. His school physics teacher rushes through rotation, so he watches a free two-hour lecture on PW’s YouTube channel. The teacher mentions the year-long JEE batch. He installs the app, takes a free mock test and scores 42 of 120. He buys the batch for about ₹4,000, joins live classes and a weekly test where he sees his all-India rank. When he photographs a doubt at 11 pm, it is answered by morning. In the summer his parents visit PW’s centre in Patna and pay about ₹80,000 for classroom coaching ({{REF:pw-flow}}).

Core loop and business model

The growth loop starts with free video: good lectures bring subscribers, subscribers install the app, tests show them where they stand, some buy a batch, and results and word of mouth bring more subscribers. The money comes in steps: cheap online batches for many, expensive classrooms for a few. In FY26 revenue rose 35% to ₹3,900 crore, paying students grew to 53.4 lakh, and the net loss shrank to ₹24 crore ({{REF:pw-growth}}). Offline students were 4.7 lakh, under a tenth, but pay many times more each. Compare Byju’s: it sold expensive tablets and courses door to door on loans; PW started free and cheap and made the classroom the premium.

Moat, in plain words. Students trust the teachers, not the app; PW’s teachers built huge free audiences first, so its cost of winning a student is low. Rivals with bigger marketing budgets couldn’t buy that trust.

Strengths

  • Very low cost to win a student
  • Teachers students trust
  • Online and offline together

Weaknesses

  • Star teachers can leave and take students
  • Centres bring rent and rules
  • Exam pressure and student wellbeing

What I’d build next

1. Students fall behind silently in a batch of 1 lakh → a weekly “you’re slipping in rotation” plan built from test answers → test scores of students flagged, a month later.

2. Doubts pile up at night → AI answers the routine ones with a worked solution; hard ones go to a teacher, with a quality check on a sample → doubts answered within an hour; share marked unhelpful.

3. Parents can’t see progress → a weekly parent summary in Hindi on WhatsApp → renewals; online-to-offline moves.
