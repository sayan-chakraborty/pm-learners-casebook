Cult.fit: selling the 7 am slot

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Arjun, 31, a product analyst in Bengaluru, has a cultpass that lets him attend classes at any centre in his city. On Sunday evening he tries to book Monday’s 7 am strength class at the HSR Layout centre. It is full, and he is third on the waitlist. At 9:04 pm two members cancel and a notification says he is in. At 6:55 am he scans a QR code at the door. The class runs 45 minutes, with each person’s effort shown on a screen. Afterwards the app asks him to rate the class and suggests Wednesday’s slot ({{REF:cult-flow}}).

Core loop and business model

The loop is book → attend → feel the class → book the next. The business is places: rent and trainers are fixed each month, so the money is made by filling every class. Cult.fit earned ₹1,216 crore in FY25 (up 31%) and cut its loss from ₹889 crore in FY24 to ₹481 crore, then ₹252 crore in FY26. By March 2026 it had 708 centres in 77 cities, and about three in four centres added in FY26 were run by franchisees ({{REF:cult-fr}}). That is the key shift: someone else pays the rent, and Cult earns a fee for the brand, the app and the booking system. It filed for an IPO in July 2026.

Moat, in plain words. A dense network of centres in big cities, a booking app that fills them, and a brand that franchisees pay to use. A single-location gym can’t offer “any centre, any day”.

Strengths

  • India’s largest centre network
  • Franchising cuts rent risk
  • Losses falling fast

Weaknesses

  • Mostly metro cities
  • Peak hours full, afternoons empty
  • Quality varies across franchisees

What I’d build next

1. Afternoons are empty while 7 am is full → a cheaper off-peak pass for students, homemakers and retirees → places filled 11 am–5 pm, without a fall in peak renewals.

2. January joiners fade by March → a four-week starter plan with a named trainer and a check-in after the third missed class → classes attended in weeks 3–6.

3. Franchise quality varies → a public score per centre from ratings, trainer turnover and cleanliness checks → gap in ratings between the best and worst centres.
