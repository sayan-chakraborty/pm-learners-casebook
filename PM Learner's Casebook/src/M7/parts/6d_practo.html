Practo: the doctor’s calendar, from both sides

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Rahul, 34, in Hyderabad, has a rash that won’t go away. He searches Practo for a skin doctor near his office and sees a list with ratings, fees and the next free slot. Dr Rao, 2 km away, charges ₹600 and has a 6:30 pm opening. He books it. At 5 pm a reminder arrives with directions. After the visit, the prescription Dr Rao typed into her clinic software appears in Rahul’s app, and he orders the cream and a blood test from it ({{REF:practo-flow}}).

Business model, and the Tata 1mg comparison

Practo earns from many small streams ({{REF:practo-money}}): clinic software subscriptions and better listings paid by doctors, online consults and tests paid by patients, and health plans sold to employers. That mix took years to pay off: ₹234 crore of revenue in FY25 and its first full year of operating profit, ₹15 crore. Tata 1mg, the comparison, is the opposite shape. It is mainly a pharmacy: it sells medicines and lab tests, with consults as a way in. That brings far more revenue, ₹2,392 crore in FY25, because each order is a basket of medicines, but thin margins and heavy delivery costs kept it at a ₹276 crore loss. Practo owns the moment a patient chooses a doctor; 1mg owns what happens after the prescription.

Moat, in plain words. Clinic software used every day by doctors gives Practo real appointment slots that a directory can’t show; millions of reviews help patients choose.

Strengths

  • Both sides: patients and clinics
  • Profitable at the operating level
  • Trusted brand for finding doctors

Weaknesses

  • Small revenue for its age
  • Hospitals and pharmacies run their own apps
  • Reviews can be gamed

What I’d build next

1. Patients arrive without their history → link ABHA records at booking so the doctor sees past reports before the visit → share of visits with records attached; consult time.

2. Small clinics still use paper → a free basic calendar with a paid pre-visit brief (old reports sorted, values out of range flagged) → clinics adopting; upgrade rate.

3. Follow-ups get forgotten → a reminder and one-tap rebooking on the date the doctor set → follow-up visits kept.
