What worked · what was missing
  • The right first question: peak and off-peak, or an average?
  • A clear plan: users → orders per user → Swiggy’s share, agreed with the interviewer.
  • Usage in four frequency groups, not one average.
  • Asked about the peak, then divided by 24 without saying so. The dinner hour carries about three times the average.
  • An arithmetic slip: 1.75 million ÷ 24 is about 73,000, printed as “70,00”.
  • Orders per user too high: the alternate-day group alone adds 1.5 orders a month. Swiggy’s own figures give about 2 orders per monthly user.
  • No check against Swiggy’s results, which publish food orders and monthly users every quarter.

Stronger answer
A
1 · Unit

Food orders delivered by Swiggy in India, not Instamart groceries. I’ll give two numbers: the average hour, for a yearly plan, and the dinner peak, because that is the hour riders and servers must be planned for.

Why this move: answering both removes the guess about what the interviewer wanted, and the peak is the number an operations team actually uses.

A
2 · Approach

Demand side, but starting from Swiggy’s own monthly users rather than India’s population. Swiggy reports about 1.9 crore monthly food users (April–June 2026), so the market-share step goes away.

Why this move: a published user count replaces the two shakiest steps, city penetration and market share.

A
3–4 · Tree and numbers

A monthly user orders about twice a month: many order once, a few order every few days. 1.9 crore × 2 = 3.8 crore orders a month ÷ 30 = about 12.7 lakh a day. ÷ 24 = 53,000 in an average hour. But orders follow meals ({{REF:hourly92}}): the 9–10 pm hour carries about 13% of the day, so the peak is 12.7 lakh × 13% ≈ 1.6 lakh orders, three times the average.

Why this move: a day’s total spread over an hourly shape gives both numbers from one tree, and makes the peak-to-average ratio visible.

A
5 · Check

Swiggy delivered 11.45 crore food orders in April–June 2026: over 91 days that is 12.6 lakh a day, or 52,000 an hour on average. My tree gives 53,000. The casebook’s 73,000 is about 40% too high, mostly because of the orders per user.

Why this move: a listed company’s quarterly orders is the strongest check there is; it confirms the tree and shows where the casebook drifted.

A
6 · Range

45,000–60,000 orders in an average hour; 1.2–1.8 lakh in the dinner peak. The peak moves most with rain, cricket finals and festivals, when a single hour can go well past 2 lakh.

Why this move: naming what spikes the peak tells an operations team when to plan extra riders.

How the answer hangs togetherAnswering both the average and the peak settled the unit. Swiggy’s published users removed the penetration and share guesses, two orders a month gave 12.7 lakh a day, and the hourly shape turned that into 53,000 average and 1.6 lakh at 9 pm. Swiggy’s quarterly orders confirmed the day, and the range says what makes the peak jump.
The reusable trick

For anything people do at set times (meals, commutes, cricket, payday), estimate the day, then ask what share falls in the busiest hour. Peak ÷ average of 2.5–3 is typical for food delivery; the peak is what you build for.
