Connect the dots

A speaker, a loyalty coin, a voter app and a tax return all earn their keep after the first transaction. The cases, the products and the technology kept returning to the same few ideas: find the moment when people act and make that moment easy; count what a unit really costs over its whole life; measure what you caused against a group that got nothing; and change live products in small, reversible steps.

Back to Lucknow: a sketch answer to the cold open

“Ananya will spend ₹25,000 on gadgets but may not spend 20 minutes voting. What would you build?” The gadgets won because Apple made each step easy and rewarding at the moment of purchase; voting asks her to register by a deadline, find a booth and set aside a morning, with no prompt at any of those moments. So don’t build a feature-rich voter app. Build the three moments. Four weeks before the registration deadline, a message: “Check you’re on the list” with a one-tap search. Three days before polling day: “Booth 14, Government School, open 7 to 6. What time will you go?”, saved to her calendar. On the morning, a reminder at the time she chose. Put the candidates’ own sworn declarations side by side, the same for everyone, and nothing that looks like a score.

“How would you know it worked?” Hold out a random group of young voters who get no messages, and compare turnout between the two groups on the official record. A 2–4 point lift among the people reached is a good result; claiming the city’s 55% will become 70% is not. Ship it the way good teams ship software: a small group first, with a pause rule (complaints of bias, wrong booth details), and switches that can stop any message in seconds.
