Guesstimates: users, and orders per hour

Both guesstimates here count people and what they do, and both trip on a word in the question. “Users” can mean everyone who ever installed an app or the people who ordered this month, and the two differ by a factor of three or more. “Per hour” can mean an average hour or the busiest one, and for food delivery the dinner hour carries three times the average. The six cards still do the rest.

1Pin down the unitMonthly users of this app, or everyone who ever ordered? An average hour, or the peak?
2Pick the approachFrom people down (demand), or from riders and kitchens (supply)?
3Draw the treePeople → who can order → who do → how often → this app’s share.
4Round numbers, one reason eachWhy 35% order monthly? Why two orders each?
5Check it another wayA company’s published users or orders, or a similar country per head.
6Give a range, and what moves itUsually how often people order, and how sharp the peak is.
Key idea · Users are not active users, and an hour is not a day ÷ 24

An app with 5 crore downloads may have 2 crore people who ordered this year and 1 crore who ordered this month. Companies report the last number (monthly transacting users) because it is the one that pays. The same care applies to time. People eat at lunch and dinner, not at 4 am, so a day’s orders divided by 24 describes an hour that never happens. Say which you are estimating: the average (for a yearly plan) or the peak (for how many riders and servers you need at 9 pm).
