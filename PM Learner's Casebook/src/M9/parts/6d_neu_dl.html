Tata Neu

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Priya, 34, in Pune, pays for ₹4,000 of groceries on BigBasket with her Tata Neu HDFC Bank credit card. The card gives her 5% back as NeuCoins, 200 coins, and BigBasket adds a 40-coin offer. Over a few months her balance reaches 1,240 coins; each coin is worth ₹1. In October she books a ₹6,500 Air India flight inside the Neu app, spends all 1,240 coins at checkout and pays the rest by card. Air India is paid for the coins she spent from the programme’s pool ({{REF:flow_neu}}).

What it is. Tata Neu launched in April 2022 as a “super-app”: one login for BigBasket, Croma, Tata CLiQ, Tata 1mg, Westside, Air India, Taj hotels, Titan and others, with NeuCoins as a shared currency and NeuPass as the loyalty membership. Tata Digital, which runs it, invested heavily and still loses money: ₹4,974 crore in FY26 on ₹35,990 crore of revenue, with BigBasket making up 64% of the loss.

What went wrong. A super-app needs a reason to open it every day, as WeChat has messaging and PhonePe has payments. Neu had none: people opened BigBasket for groceries and Air India for flights directly, and saw little reason to go through Neu. The brands underneath ran their own apps, prices and offers, so the experience felt stitched together. Coins were earned but often not spent, which made them feel worth less than ₹1.

What worked, and the change. The co-branded card did. It had passed 20 lakh cards by March 2025, took over 13% of new credit cards issued in one quarter, and carried more than ₹800 crore a month of UPI spends. Card users earn coins everywhere, so the coins build up fast enough to matter. By 2025–26 Tata Digital had refocused Neu on financial services (the card, payments, loans and insurance) and loyalty, rather than on being a shopping mall.

North StarMembers who earn and spend coins across two or more brands in 90 days
InputsActive cards; coins earned per member; coins spent ÷ coins earned; first purchase at a new brand
GuardrailsCost of coins as a share of sales; card defaults; complaints about missing coins

Moat, in plain words. Trusted brands that people already buy from, and a card that turns everyday spending into coins for them. Weakness: no daily habit of its own, and brands that answer to their own profit targets.

Build next → Problem: coins sit unspent and feel worthless. Bet: “pay with coins” at the counter of every Tata store and café, suggested automatically when the balance covers the bill. Metric: coins spent ÷ coins earned; members spending at a second brand.

DigiLocker (with ONDC for comparison)

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Ananya’s grandfather, 71, is stopped at a traffic check in Lucknow without his licence. He opens DigiLocker, which he set up last year with an Aadhaar OTP; the app had then fetched his driving licence straight from the transport department’s records. He shows it; the constable scans its QR code with the police app, which checks the record live: valid, not suspended. He is on his way in two minutes ({{REF:flow_dl}}).

How it works, and why it is trusted. DigiLocker, launched in 2015 by the government’s digital-services arm, holds two kinds of document ({{REF:dl_issued}}). Issued documents are fetched from the body that issued them (CBSE mark sheets, driving licences, vehicle registrations, PAN, insurance policies), carry its digital signature, and under the IT rules count the same as the original. Uploaded documents are scans a citizen adds for their own use, with no special standing. By March 2026 DigiLocker had 67.6 crore users, and over 950 crore documents had been issued through it. Banks use it for KYC, universities for admissions, and employers to check degrees, each with the citizen’s consent.

Business model. None in the usual sense: the state pays, and the return is fewer office visits, fewer forged certificates and faster checks. The costs a PM watches are uptime at peak times (results day for board exams), how many issuers are connected, and help for people who get locked out when their Aadhaar-linked number changes. ONDC, for comparison, is the same idea applied to shopping: an open network (launched 2022) where any buyer app can show any seller’s goods, so a kirana shop on one app can sell to a customer on another. Where DigiLocker succeeded because a government department is the only issuer of a licence, ONDC must persuade private buyers and sellers to join, and orders have been harder to sustain once early discounts ended.

North StarChecks done with an issued document instead of a paper copy, each month
InputsIssuers connected; documents issued; verifiers (banks, police, colleges) accepting it; sign-ups that fetch a document
GuardrailsUptime on results day; wrong or stale records; access requests without consent

Build next → Problem: many of the elderly can’t manage the app when a document is needed. Bet: a “family helper” mode where a child can show a parent’s document, with the parent’s one-time consent and a log the parent can see. Metric: checks completed for users over 60; consent revocations.

In the interview · How these products come up
  • “Improve TurboTax.” It is used once a year and feared; start with the step where people drop out or pay for help, and remember trust is the product.
  • “Should QuickBooks re-enter India?” Ask what the local rules and channel are (GST, Tally, accountants) and whether Intuit can win them; a global product alone lost last time.
  • “How does a free app like Credit Karma make money, and what can go wrong?” Lenders pay per approval; the risk is ranking offers by fee instead of by the member’s chances.
  • “Why did Tata Neu struggle, and what would you change?” No daily reason to open it; build around what worked (the card) and make coins easy to spend everywhere.
