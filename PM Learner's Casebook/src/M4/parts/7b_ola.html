Ola: how a market leader loses a two-sided market

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Ravi, 34, books an Ola auto from Koramangala to MG Road. Ola’s own map, Ola Maps, estimates 6 km, ₹120 and 22 minutes. A driver 800 m away accepts; Ravi gets an OTP and an ETA of 4 minutes. At the end he pays ₹120 by UPI, and all of it goes to the driver. Ola earns nothing on this ride. It earns once a day, from the flat fee the driver pays to stay on the platform ({{REF:ol-walk}}).

The loop, running backwards

Ola had more of India’s cab market than Uber in the mid-2010s. By FY25 its ride-hailing revenue had fallen 42% to ₹1,171 Cr, with a loss of ₹662 Cr; a write-down of its Ola Electric stake pushed the group loss to about ₹1,975 Cr. The causes are a lesson in two-sided markets. Leadership time and money went to Ola Electric and the AI venture Krutrim. Drivers complained about payouts and incentives and drifted to rivals. Fewer drivers meant longer waits and more cancellations, and riders followed. The same loop that grows a marketplace shrinks it just as fast ({{REF:ol-loop}}).

Business model. Now mainly flat fees from drivers, plus fees on cab rentals and outstation trips. Revenue no longer rises with rides, so it must rise with active drivers or new products. Moat, in plain words. Thin today: a known brand and its own map. The map is a real asset: it removes a cost every rival pays and could be sold to other companies.

Strengths

  • Its own map: a lower cost per trip
  • A brand every Indian rider knows
  • All vehicle types in one app

Weaknesses

  • Lost driver trust; weak service reputation
  • Late to subscriptions, so no counter-position left
  • Thin cash for a price or incentive war

What I’d build next (a turnaround)

1. Supply is the constraint → a guaranteed minimum hourly earning for 90 days in five core cities, funded by pausing weak cities → drivers online at peak; ETA.

2. Returning riders remember bad experiences → an automatic ₹50 credit whenever a driver cancels → 30-day retention of returning riders; driver cancellations.

3. Ola Maps is a cost saver nobody outside uses → sell it to delivery and logistics firms with Indian-address features → API revenue; external developers.
