Uber: a pricing and matching engine that happens to move cars

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Ananya, from the cold open, finally gets a car. She types Jayanagar; the app shows an upfront fare of ₹610 at 1.8× surge before she commits. She confirms. In the next two seconds Uber’s dispatch ranks nearby drivers by how soon each could reach her, and offers the ride to Suresh, 1.2 km away, who sees the drop area and accepts. Ananya gets his name, plate and a four-digit PIN. When he arrives she reads out the PIN, the trip starts, and at the end ₹610 leaves her UPI account. Suresh sees ₹488 in his weekly payout ({{REF:ub-walk}}).

Core loop: liquidity

Uber’s whole strategy is one loop ({{REF:ub-loop}}). More riders in an area mean drivers earn more per hour, so more drivers log on. More drivers mean a car is always close, so pickups are quicker and drivers spend less time driving empty. Less empty driving means Uber can hold fares steady and still pay drivers well, which brings more riders. The loop is local: winning Mumbai does nothing for Indore. That is why rivals can attack city by city.

Business model, with numbers

Uber doesn’t report India separately. Worldwide, in April–June 2026, riders and eaters paid $58.0 bn (+22%); Uber kept $14.2 bn as revenue and earned $2.8 bn of adjusted EBITDA, on 3.9 bn trips and 208 mn monthly users ({{REF:ub-earn}}). In India it runs two models side by side: commission on cabs (capped at 20% for driver-owned cars under the 2025 rules) and a daily subscription for autos since February 2025.

Moat, in plain words. Density in each big city, which makes pickups faster than a smaller rival can offer. Global pricing, mapping and safety technology that a local player can’t afford to build. And a brand that corporate travel desks trust. The weakness: drivers and riders both use several apps, so the moat must be re-earned every evening.

Strengths

  • Market leader in cabs and autos
  • Profitable worldwide, so it can outspend locals
  • Trusted by companies and airport travellers

Weaknesses

  • Priced above Rapido and Namma Yatri for autos and bikes
  • Cancellations and surge anger riders at peaks
  • Rules on fares and fees now vary state by state

What I’d build next

1. Office-goers pay surge on the same route at 9 am every day → a weekly commute pass: fixed route and time, a fixed price below average surge, guaranteed pickup → weekday trips per pass holder; driver utilisation at peak.

2. Drivers cancel trips that end far away → a return premium on edge trips, offered first to drivers heading that way → completed trips per request on edge trips.

3. Families don’t trust bike taxis for teenagers → a “trusted rider” mode: verified drivers with top ratings, live sharing to a parent, no cash → share of under-21 riders; safety reports.
