Guesstimates: sizing a fleet

Mobility guesstimates usually ask about supply: how many drivers, bikes, buses or servers. The trap is to count people instead of capacity. A fleet is sized for its busiest hour, and one vehicle can serve only so many trips in that hour. The six cards still do the work.

1Pin down the unitDrivers registered, active this month, or online right now? Cars only, or autos and bikes too?
2Pick the approachFor fleets: supply from demand. Trips at the peak ÷ trips one vehicle does in that hour.
3Draw the treePeople × share riding × trips → peak-hour trips → ÷ trips per vehicle per hour → ÷ share online at peak.
4Round numbers, one reason eachA trip cycle includes the empty drive to the pickup, not just the ride.
5Check it another wayTrips per driver per day, or trips per resident per day. Both have sensible ranges.
6Give a range, and what moves itUsually the share of people who ride, or the peak share of the day.
Key idea · A fleet is sized for its busiest hour

Fleet = peak-hour trips ÷ trips one vehicle completes in an hour, plus spares. If a quarter of a day’s rides fall in two morning hours, sizing on the daily average leaves riders stranded at 9 am and vehicles idle at 3 pm. Every mobility business, and every server farm, is a peak problem first.
