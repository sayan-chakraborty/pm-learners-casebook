Google Maps: two billion users who are also its sensors

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Meera, 41, needs to reach Bengaluru airport from Indiranagar for a 9 pm flight. At 6:30 pm Maps offers three routes; the fastest takes 52 minutes via Outer Ring Road. Twenty minutes in, someone reports a crash on the Hebbal flyover; Maps reroutes her and saves nine minutes. A café on the new route appears as a labelled ad. None of this needs a single traffic camera: the speeds come from millions of other phones moving through the same roads ({{REF:mp-walk}}).

Core loop: data

Maps improves because people use it ({{REF:mp-loop}}). More drivers mean more speed readings on more roads, so ETAs get more accurate, so more people trust Maps and use it. The same holds for places: more users write more reviews and upload more photos, which make search results better. A new map can copy the roads; it can’t copy a decade of live readings and reviews.

Business model. Maps is free to users. Alphabet doesn’t report its revenue, but it earns in three ways: local ads (promoted pins and sponsored results when people search “near me”); Google Maps Platform (companies pay per 1,000 calls to show maps, routes and places in their own apps); and feeding Search, where local intent makes ads more valuable. The biggest customers of the Platform, ride and delivery apps, are also the ones most likely to build their own map, as Ola did.

Moat, in plain words. The live data from two billion users, years of Street View imagery and place reviews, and the default spot on most Android phones.

Strengths

  • Unmatched live traffic and place data
  • Default on Android; 2 bn+ users
  • AI answers built on real reviews

Weaknesses

  • Indian addresses and pins still go wrong
  • Big API customers build their own maps
  • Ads in local results can erode trust

What I’d build next

1. Delivery riders lose minutes finding the right gate → gate-level pins learned from where past deliveries actually ended → arrival-to-door time for Platform customers.

2. Tourists don’t know what’s worth seeing in two days → “Plan my day” from saved places, open hours and travel time → trips with a plan followed.

3. Commuters switch between metro, auto and walking → one ticket and one route across modes via ONDC → multi-mode trips booked in Maps.
