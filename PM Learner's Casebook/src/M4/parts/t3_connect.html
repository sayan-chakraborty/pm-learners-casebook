Back to Outer Ring Road · A sketch answer for the rainy-Friday question

Pricing, matching or drivers? Start by splitting the failures. Are riders seeing “no drivers found” (supply is short), or are drivers accepting and cancelling (the trips are wrong for them)? Tonight both happen, but for different reasons.

Supply is short because the rain slows everything. A 35-minute trip now takes 55, so each driver completes fewer trips an hour; the same drivers serve about a third fewer riders. Surge at 1.8× is doing its job if it pulls drivers in from dry areas within 10–15 minutes. If it doesn’t, the cap (2× base) may be too low to close the gap, and a longer honest wait is better than a spinning wheel.

Cancellations are the driver’s maths. Jayanagar at 7 pm in the rain means a long crawl back. Split cancellations by drop area and time; if edge and long trips dominate, add a return premium and offer those trips first to drivers heading that way.

And the system must stay up. Rain triples requests in 20 minutes. Keep matching consistent, serve cached ETAs if pricing struggles, and switch off promos before bookings suffer.

Goal (North Star): completed trips per request tonight. Safety checks (guardrails): driver earnings per online hour; rider complaints about surge; booking success rate against the SLO. Trade-off: a higher price clears the queue but angers riders; a capped price keeps goodwill but leaves some riders without a car.

Key idea · Every mobility problem is a peak problem

Drivers, bikes, school vans and servers are all capacity, and capacity is set by the busiest hour, not the average one. When you size a fleet, price a ride or plan a launch night, ask first: what happens at 9 am, at 6 pm in the rain, and at midnight on 31 December?
