R1 (Resume): The first round was a basic information and resume-related round. 
He asked me various resume-based questions and checked my basic

round was basically around that only, and it lasted for around 20 to 25 minutes. 
R2 (Product Strategy, Guesstimate, Technical):  
Then came the second round, which I think was the breakthrough round. 
The second round was about 1.5 hours, roughly 1 hour 25–30 minutes in 
total. It revolved entirely around my understanding of B2B SaaS. He 
initially started with a question like, what do you understand about B2B 

=== p73
SaaS as a concept? Since I was very open with him and mentioned that I 
hadn’t worked in SaaS but had worked on a B2C product, while still having 
some knowledge of SaaS, he drilled deeper into the basics, such as what 
metrics you look at while evaluating a SaaS business, etc. 
I had prepared basic SaaS concepts and how B2B SaaS works, so I was 
able to explain aspects like acquisition, how the sales cycle works, PLG-
related growth, service attribution, and related metrics. Once he 
understood what I knew, he asked me a question related to conversions 
and reach. Essentially, he was trying to put me into a product strategy role 
and assess how I structure my thoughts. 
There was no formally structured case. Instead, we were building the case 
as we went along. For example, he asked how I would approach a client 
and pitch BrowserStack’s product, what components BrowserStack 
currently has and whether I was aware of them. First, he asked about my 
understanding of the products, and I told him that I had researched what 
products they offer. Then he said, okay, now assume you must pitch one 
of these products to a client. How will you do it? That was case one. 
Case two was something along the lines of We have an existing customer; 
how would you ensure retention? He also asked about what metrics I 
would look at if the customer was trying to switch away. So, we went step 
by step through various stages such as customer onboarding, the trial 
phase of a SaaS product, customer engagement, and the factors we look 
at not just company-specific factors, but also competition and broader 
market considerations. He was trying to see whether my structured way of 
thinking connected company-level decisions with market competition and 
macroeconomic factors as well. 
There was also one small guesstimate. For me, it wasn’t a full-fledged

B2C 
a. Consumer Software

4. Design a hyperlocal news app for rural India.  
5. Design a product that helps users reduce unnecessary online shopping. Assume the

6. Design a digital experience for attending a large live event such as a concert or 
sporting event.  
7. Design a product that helps elderly users independently manage routine digital tasks 
such as payments, bookings, and appointments.  
8. Design a product for households that want to reduce their electricity consumption 
without significantly changing their lifestyle. Identify the highest-value intervention 
and explain the behavioural challenge you are solving. 
b. Physical Products/IoT 
9. Design an intelligent shoe for visually impaired users. 
 
B2B 
10. Design a product that helps small retailers understand which products are likely to 
run out of stock in the coming week.  
11. Design a product that helps sales representatives prepare for their customer 
meetings. Decide which part of the sales workflow you would solve first. 
12. Design a product for independent restaurants to manage their supplier orders.  
13. Design a product for independent healthcare clinics to reduce the administrative 
work involved in managing appointments and follow-ups.  
14. Design an AI copilot for procurement teams that helps them evaluate supplier 
proposals. Explain what decisions the AI should assist with and where human 
approval should remain mandatory.
