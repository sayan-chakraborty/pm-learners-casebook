Question 6 · A conflict or disagreement13 rounds
As actually askedtell me about a time when you disagreed with your manager, both an example where I was right and one where my manager was right (Ethos) · Strengths? Weaknesses? Conflict in the past? (Cashfree) · How do you deal with folks disagreeing with you? (2026 report, p. 108)

What they’re checking. Whether you can disagree without making it personal, and whether you can lose an argument gracefully. The Ethos version is clever: it asks for one you won and one you lost. Prepare both. The move that works in almost every conflict story is to step back from the two positions to the goal both sides share, then find a small test that settles the worry ({{REF:conflict}}).

Example answer (written for this guide) · pricing analyst at an edtech company
A

I’d built a simpler pricing page and wanted it live in all cities on 1 March, before admissions season. My manager wanted to wait till April, because the sales team hadn’t been trained on the new plans and she expected complaints. We went back and forth twice. Then I asked what we both wanted, and it was the same thing: more paid sign-ups without angry parents. So I suggested two cities on 1 March, a one-page FAQ for sales, and all cities in April if complaints stayed under 2%.

Q

And? Were you right?

A

Partly. Sign-ups in the two cities rose 11%. But complaints hit 3% in the first week, on one plan name that confused people, so she was right about that. We renamed it, and it went national in April. I’d still push for the early test. I’d also have shown the plan names to three salespeople first.

Why this move: admitting the manager was partly right makes the story believable, and covers the “one where your manager was right” half of the question.

Weak line
“She just didn’t understand the data, so I escalated to her boss.”
Better line
“We were arguing about dates. Once we agreed the goal, the test we ran settled it.”
Question 7 · Strengths and weaknesses12 rounds, often in HR and founder rounds
As actually askedquality you would hate seeing in your teammates (Paytm) · What is your superpower? How much would you rate yourself on hard work? (Winzo, founder round) · weaknesses and what I am doing to improve them (a CXO stress round, p. 81) · What are your weaknesses that can obstruct your performance as a Product Manager? (p. 162)

What they’re checking. Self-awareness. A strength with no proof sounds like a claim. A weakness that is really a strength (“I work too hard”) tells them you won’t admit anything, which is itself a weakness. Pick a real one that matters in a PM job but won’t sink you, show when it cost you, and what you now do about it.

Example answer (written for this guide) · fresher, engineering graduate with one internship
A

My strength is turning a messy problem into one clear page. In my internship, five teams had different numbers for the same funnel; I wrote a one-page definition of each step that all five signed off, and it’s still in use. My weakness is that I hold on to work too long before showing it. In the same internship I spent a week polishing an analysis that my manager redirected in ten minutes. Now I share a rough draft by day two, even if it looks unfinished. In my last three projects I got the key correction in the first week instead of the last.

Q

Name one quality you’d hate in a teammate.

A

Saying “done” when it isn’t. I can plan around a delay I know about. I can’t plan around one I find out about on launch day.

Why this move: the Paytm question is the weakness question turned around. Answer with a behaviour, not a personality type, and give the reason in one line.

Question 8 · A failure, or a time you were criticisednamed in 5 rounds; the least-prepared story
As actually askedWhat was some time when you were criticized? · As a team, give an instance when you failed · Did you criticize anyone and were you criticised by anyone? (Atlassian) · Do you follow the do it right first-time approach or fail fast approach? (Dream11)

What they’re checking. Whether you own your part, and whether anything changed afterwards. The usual weak answer fails on both: all “we”, and it ends at “it all worked out”. A good failure story is a curve ({{REF:failarc}}): it goes down, you act, and it comes back up because you changed how you work, with proof from the next time.

From a real interview · Mastercard, 2026

A candidate mentioned a startup on their resume. The interviewer asked: if you turned down every offer and started again, what would you do? The candidate listed steps any textbook would give: market research, demand estimation, finding white space, the problem to solve. The report says the interviewer “wasn’t very impressed”, and the interview ended there. A question about your own past venture wants your lessons: what you got wrong the first time and what you’d do differently, not a general method. ISB BTC Handbook 2026, p. 118. Summarised.

Example answer (written for this guide) · fresher, college-fest sponsorship
A

I led sponsorship for our college fest, with a ₹4 lakh target. I was late sending our deck to our biggest sponsor, by two weeks, because I kept improving it. By the time it arrived, their budget had gone to another college. That cost us ₹1.5 lakh. That week I called 11 smaller local sponsors and brought back ₹90,000, so we ended at about 85% of target. The lasting fix was a shared tracker with a deadline and one owner for every sponsor, and a rule that a deck goes out when it’s good enough, not perfect. The next year, as a mentor, I saw all nine decks go out three weeks early.

Q

So do you do it right first time, or fail fast?

A

It depends on the cost of being wrong. A sponsor deck, fail fast and fix. A payment flow that moves people’s money, get it right first.

Why this move: “it depends” is only a good answer if you say on what. The cost of a mistake decides it.

Question 9 · Leading or persuading without authority8 rounds, plus many “convince” follow-ups
As actually askedIf you have to convince your founder to prioritise this solution, how … Now, you have to convince your CFO to give money for it (Salesforce) · the candidate had to convince an engineer to take on a project (p. 143) · leadership skills, evidence of proactiveness (a hiring-manager round, p. 82)

What they’re checking. A PM has almost no one reporting to them, so all their work is persuasion. They want to see that you understood what the other person cared about and made it easy for them to say yes.

From a real interview · Salesforce, 2026

After the candidate argued for a solution, the interviewer asked how they would convince the founder, then the CFO. The candidate kept the same reasoning for the CFO because they still thought it held, and said so. A third follow-up then changed the conditions (low barriers to entry, competitors you can’t beat), and the candidate changed their plan to match. Keeping your view when only the audience changes, and updating it when the facts change, is the skill being tested. ISB BTC Handbook 2026, p. 113. Summarised.

Example answer (written for this guide) · business role, the stock-tracker story
A

I needed 30 depot managers, none of whom reported to me, to fill in a new stock tracker every day. Their first reaction was “one more report”. So I visited six depots and asked what they hated about month-end. The answer was the calls from sales asking “do you have stock?”. I added a view that sales could check themselves, which cut those calls, and I took one old daily form off their plate. I started with the two depot managers others listened to. After their pilot they told the rest at the monthly review, which worked better than anything I could have said. 28 of 30 were using it within eight weeks.

Why this move: you persuade by removing work and giving people something they want, and you let respected peers carry the message.

Question 10 · Where do you see yourself in five years?7 rounds
As actually askedwhy PM, why Flipkart, where do you see yourself in 5 years (Flipkart) · What’s your goal in next 5 years (Housing.com) · What are your long-term aspirations, and how does joining Affinity play a part in that? (Affinity Global)

What they’re checking. Whether this job is a real step for you or a stopgap, and whether you’ll stay. Name the skill you want to build first, the scope you want later, and how this job connects the two. Avoid titles (“I want to be a VP”) and avoid anything that sounds like leaving (“start my own company in two years”).

Example answer (written for this guide) · any background
A

For the first two years, I want to get very good at the basics: shipping small things fast, and learning to read the data on what they did. In five years I’d like to own a whole product area, say the seller side of a marketplace, where I decide the roadmap and answer for its numbers. This role is the right start if new PMs here ship to real users early, as your team described at the pre-placement talk. Shipping and then reading the results is the fastest way I know to learn. (Say this only if it’s true of that company.)

Question 11 · How do you use AI in your work?16 rounds, new this year
As actually askedHow do you use vibe coding tools like Replit/Lovable in your daily life? · Which is your favorite AI product? Why? (Affinity Global) · how I use AI in day-to-day (a hiring-manager round, p. 82)

What they’re checking. Whether you actually use these tools and know where they go wrong. Name two or three specific habits, one thing you built or saved, and one limit you’ve hit. If it’s on your resume, expect to show it.

From a real interview · HiLabs, 2026

Before defending their case deck, one candidate asked ChatGPT to generate more questions an interviewer might ask about it, and prepared answers. In the round, they also kept naming the risk and the fix for everything they proposed. The interviewer praised the discussion at the end. ISB BTC Handbook 2026, p. 83. Summarised.

Example answer (written for this guide) · MBA student
A

Three ways. I paste in app-store reviews and ask for the main complaints, then I read 20 of the reviews myself to check it hasn’t made any up. I built a small prototype of a budget tracker on Lovable in an evening to test an idea with five friends before writing anything down. And I use it to argue against my own case answers. The limit I hit: for Indian market numbers it will confidently give figures with no source, so I never use one without checking it.
