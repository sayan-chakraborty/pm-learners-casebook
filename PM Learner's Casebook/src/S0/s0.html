How UPI moves money

7:40 pm, a kirana in Pune. Asha buys ₹500 of groceries, scans the QR code at the counter and types her UPI PIN. Before she has put her phone away, the shop’s soundbox says “₹500 received”. PhonePe was on her screen the whole time, yet PhonePe never held her ₹500. Here is who did.

24.51 bnUPI payments in Aug 2026, up 22% on Aug 2025
791 mnpayments a day, about 9,150 every second on average
₹29.82 lakh Crmoved over UPI in Aug 2026
45.9% · 32.4%PhonePe · Google Pay share of UPI volume; Paytm 8.1%

UPI ID (or VPA, virtual payment address) is a nickname for a bank account, such as kirana@ybl. A TPAP (third-party app provider) such as PhonePe or Google Pay builds the screens but cannot join UPI alone; it plugs in through one or more PSP banks (payment service providers). NPCI, owned by a group of banks, runs the switch. The issuer is the payer’s bank and the acquirer is the merchant’s.

Who pays for a free payment?

Since January 2020 merchants have paid zero MDR on UPI (merchant discount rate: the share of each payment a merchant pays to accept it; roughly 2% on a credit card). Banks and apps carried the cost, and the government paid a small subsidy instead: ₹1,500 Cr in FY25, at 0.15% on small-merchant payments up to ₹2,000. Zero MDR is why a chaiwala takes UPI but not cards. It is also why UPI apps lose money on payments. That changes on 15 October 2026, when a 0.4% MDR starts on large merchants’ payments above ₹2,000.

Check · “PhonePe must cut its share to 30% by January 2027” Overstated

The rule, announced in November 2020, says an app above 30% of UPI volume (rolling three months) must stop onboarding new users. It does not force existing users off. NPCI has pushed the deadline back three times, most recently to 31 December 2026, and as of September 2026 it is not in force. Watch it: a cap on new users would hand growth to Paytm, Navi and CRED.

Key idea · Payments are the hook

With most UPI payments free to the merchant, apps earn from what the payment habit lets them sell: loans, insurance, broking, bill payments, ads and soundbox rentals. PhonePe’s lending arm earned ₹945 Cr in FY26, 2.5× the year before.

In the interview

Asked “How does PhonePe make money?”, avoid “transaction fees”. Say: “Most UPI payments earn it close to nothing. The habit is distribution for loans, insurance and bills. From October, large-merchant payments above ₹2,000 add a fee line, but the split isn’t public yet.”

What worked · what was missing

  • Pinned the definition (unique users, at least one open a day) and checked new vs existing users.
  • Segmented by OS version and maker, which turned “Android” into “Android 12+”.
  • Closed the loose end. The North America skew didn’t fit a pure OS bug, so the candidate proposed a staged rollout and asked for proof. This is the best moment in the case.
  • Never asked “sudden or slow?” A sudden drop usually means something broke; a slow one means users are drifting away. That one question points straight at the update and saves three turns.
  • Looked outside first, although the drop began right after our own update.
  • Never checked the size. Does the crash explain all 40%, or is something else going on too?

Check · “Roll back to the last stable version” Overstated

On Android you can’t take an update back from phones that already have it. You can only stop it reaching more phones (the Play Store lets you pause a gradual release) and then send a newer version containing the old, working code. Teams that put risky features behind an on/off switch controlled from their own servers (a “feature flag”) can simply switch the feature off. More in M9.

The scoping kit · five questions before any answer

A good doctor doesn’t write a prescription the moment you say “headache”. They ask where it hurts, since when, and whether anything changed: new food, new medicine, less sleep. The scoping kit is your version of those questions. It takes about a minute and stops you solving the wrong problem.

Same questions, everyday setting: planning a friend’s birthday, you’d ask what they’d enjoy (goal), who is coming (who), what’s already booked (today), and the budget and date (limits).

1What exactly is it?So you and the interviewer mean the same thing.“Android daily users down 40% in two weeks, iPhone steady. Right?”✓ asked

Rule of thumb: don’t ask everything. Ask 3–4, say one assumption out loud (“I’ll assume this is worldwide”), and begin.

The number is real and nothing outside changed. For users on Android 12 or newer who installed the update, the app crashes the moment they open it.

Why this move: clear the cheap explanations in one breath, then narrow down to exactly who is hit.

A
④ Check the size

Does that explain the whole 40%? Say 70% of our Android users are on Android 12 or newer, and 60% of them have updated (illustrative). 70% × 60% = 42% who can’t open the app. That’s close to 40%, so this one bug explains the drop. North America saw it first because the update reached it first.

Why this move: if the numbers didn’t add up, there would be a second cause still to find.

Q

Good. What do you do now, and how will you know it’s fixed?

A
⑤ Fix · ⑥ Measure

Today: stop the update reaching more phones. Send out a fixed version fast, because on Android you can’t take an update back. Text affected users to update, since they can’t open the app to read a notice.

Goal (North Star): Android daily users back to normal within a week. Safety check (guardrail): logins and payments still work after the rushed fix. Trade-off: a rushed fix is less tested, so it goes to 10% of users for a few hours before everyone. Next time: test every release on the newest Android versions.

Framework · Finding the cause: four doors, then 5 Whys

1 · The situation.A number has dropped and you have 25 minutes. The usual mistake is to grab the first idea (“a rival must have run an offer”) and spend the case defending it.

2 · An everyday example.Your scooter won’t start. You don’t open up the engine first. You check the easy things first, in an order that works just as well for an app:

DoorThe questionScooter won’t startAndroid users fell 40%

2 · OutsideDid the world change?Is there a petrol strike?Did a rival launch an offer?

3 · The framework.Knock on the doors in order, because the first ones are quickest to check. If a clue points at one door, go there early: a sudden drop right after an update points at door 3. When a door opens, keep asking “why?” (the 5 Whys) until you reach something a team can fix. That is usually a missing step in a process, not a person to blame.

4 · When it misleads.Your scooter has a flat tyre and an empty tank. Fix the tyre and it still won’t go. So once you find a cause, check it explains the whole drop (move ④ above). 5 Whys also goes wrong if you stop at “the developer made a mistake”. People always make mistakes; the fix is the check that should have caught this one.

5 · In the interview.Say it once, in plain words: “First I’ll check the number is real, then whether something outside changed, then what we changed, then which users are hit.” Then follow the clues, and say out loud when you skip a door and why.

M1 · Technology · System design I (preview)

2 seconds inside a UPI payment

Back at the kirana. Between Asha’s thumb on “Pay” and the soundbox speaking, about ten messages cross five organisations. Knowing that path lets a PM answer the question every payments team hears weekly: “The money left my account but the shop didn’t get it. Where is it?”

API: a fixed menu of requests one system accepts from another. In UPI, ReqPay means “pay this”.

Switch: a router for payment messages. NPCI’s switch sees every UPI payment and holds none of the money.

Timeout: how long a system waits before giving up. UPI allows 15 s for pay requests and 10 s for status checks.

Idempotency: sending the same request twice has the effect of sending it once. Each UPI payment carries a unique ID, so a retry can’t debit twice.

Pending (“deemed”): debit done, credit not confirmed. A status check or a reversal settles it later.

p95 latency: the time within which 95% of requests finish.

Why the average lies

Take 100 payments: 90 finish in 1.2 s, 8 take 3 s and 2 hang until the 15 s timeout. The mean is (90×1.2 + 8×3 + 2×15) ÷ 100 = 1.62 s, which looks healthy. But the 95th payment in line (p95) takes 3 s and the 99th (p99) takes 15 s. Users feel their own payment, not the average: the unlucky two stand at the counter staring at a spinner. At 791 mn payments a day, 2 in 100 would be 1.6 crore stuck payments daily (illustrative; real UPI failure rates are lower).

Success rates multiply

A payment succeeds only if every hop succeeds, so the hop success rates multiply. Five hops at 99.9% each give 0.9995 ≈ 99.5%: one payment in 200 fails, although no single hop looks bad. Let one issuer bank slip to 99.0% and the chain drops to 0.9994 × 0.99 ≈ 98.6%, or one in 72. That is why payments teams track success rate per bank, and why an app’s success rate can fall with no change to the app.

*At Aug 2026’s average of ~9,150 payments a second (791 mn ÷ 86,400 s). Peaks run higher, so real losses would be larger. Each extra nine cuts downtime tenfold and usually costs far more than ten times as much.

When it breaks · “Money gone, shop not paid”

If step ⑤ debits Asha but the reply to ⑦ or ⑧ is lost, the payment is pending. The app must not say “failed” (Asha pays again and is out ₹1,000) or “success” (the kirana hands over goods unpaid). RBI’s rule: a failed UPI payment that debited the customer must be reversed by T+1, or the bank pays ₹100 a day.

Key idea · The PM decisions this drives
  • What the pending screen says, and how often it re-checks status.
  • Retry rules: re-check status freely, never resend a payment with a new ID.
  • Routing: PhonePe spreads users across PSP banks (@ybl, @ibl, @axl), so a failing bank can be routed around.
  • Dashboards: success rate and p95 per bank, not one average.
In the interview

“UPI failures are up on our app. Why?” Split by hop before you guess: one issuer bank, one PSP bank, or all of them? Failures clustered on one bank point outside your app. Failures everywhere point at your app, or at NPCI.

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Meena, 41, runs a tailoring shop in Indore. On the 5th of each month she opens PhonePe, taps Electricity, picks her board, and the app fetches her ₹1,240 bill through Bharat BillPay. One PIN later it is paid, and a banner offers her a business loan. That banner is where PhonePe’s revenue is heading.
