Real-life example · Tearing down any B2B product in five questions, on Zoho

Interviewers often name a business product you haven’t studied. Five questions get you to a sensible teardown in two minutes. Here they are on Zoho, the Chennai company that earned ₹12,313 crore in FY25 without outside investors.

  1. Who buys, who uses, who can say no? Small and mid-sized business owners and IT heads buy; their sales, accounts and support staff use it daily; the finance head watches the bill.
  2. How does it land and expand? One app first, often Zoho Mail, CRM or Books (accounting), then more apps, then the Zoho One bundle of dozens of apps priced per employee.
  3. How is it priced, and what does it cost to serve? Per user or per employee a month; Zoho runs its own data centres, which keeps its cost low and lets it undercut rivals.
  4. What is the moat? Many apps that share one set of customer data, so leaving means moving everything; and prices well under Salesforce and Microsoft for the same jobs.
  5. What does AI change? Its assistant, Zia, adds a cost to every question; a per-employee bundle price must now cover heavy AI users too.

The same five questions work for Freshworks, Postman or any SaaS name you are given. They also give you the right place to start a “what would you build next?” answer: the step where the product is weakest.

In the interview · How these products come up
  • “AWS, Azure or Google Cloud for our start-up?” Start from the team’s skills, the credits on offer and where the data must live; the AI models each hosts now matter too.
  • “Design a feature for Amplitude.” Its users are PMs and analysts; start from a question they can’t answer today, and check the data behind it is clean.
  • “Why do plain-words analytics tools give wrong answers?” Usually the definitions, not the AI: “sales” meant gross to one team and net to another.
  • “Should Copilot be priced per seat?” Compare cost per question with the seat price for light and heavy users; propose a seat with an allowance, or agents per use.
