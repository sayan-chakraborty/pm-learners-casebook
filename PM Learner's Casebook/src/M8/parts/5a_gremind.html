Guesstimates: how much storage?

Cloud products are built on disks, and a PM for Drive, Photos or any file tool will be asked how much storage something needs. Both guesstimates here are about that. They go wrong in the same two ways. People assume a free allowance is filled, when most users store a small part of it and a few fill it up. And they forget that a cloud company never keeps just one copy of a file. The six cards still do the rest.

1Pin down the unitData users store, or disk the company must buy? One year, or everything so far?
2Pick the approachFrom users and what each stores (demand), or from data centres (supply)?
3Draw the treeUsers → items each → size of each → copies kept.
4Round numbers, one reason eachWhy 2 MB a photo? Why 20 GB used of 200?
5Check it another wayAgainst the company’s own published photo or user counts.
6Give a range, and what moves itUsually average use, and file size after compression.
Key idea · Stored is not the same as offered

Give 100 people a 200 GB allowance and about 70 will store under 5 GB, while two fill it to the brim. The average is closer to 15–20 GB than to 120 GB. The opposite mistake happens on the supply side: every file a user stores sits on disk more than once (copies in other buildings and spare pieces to rebuild lost data), and the company buys disks months ahead of need. So size what people actually store, then multiply by what the company keeps for each byte.
