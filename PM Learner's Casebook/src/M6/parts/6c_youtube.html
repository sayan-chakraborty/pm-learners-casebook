YouTube: India’s free television, made by its viewers

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Karthik, 34, ran a two-wheeler garage in Coimbatore. He films a 12-minute Tamil video on cleaning a carburettor and uploads it one night. YouTube makes several sizes of it, adds captions and an automatic Hindi dub, and checks the background song against music owned by labels. Then it shows the video to a few thousand people who watch bike-repair videos. They watch nine minutes of twelve on average, so YouTube shows it to more. In a month it has 1 lakh views. Karthik earns about ₹2,640 from the ads on it, more from a tool brand’s sponsorship, and more again from channel members who pay him monthly ({{REF:yt-flow}}).

Business model, with numbers. Mostly ads: YouTube’s ad revenue was $36 bn in 2024 and grew 13% in April–June 2026. Then subscriptions (Premium, Music and, in the US, YouTube TV) and a share of fan payments. YouTube says it paid creators, artists and media companies more than $100 bn from 2021 to 2024. India has about 52 crore YouTube users, more than any other country, but Indian ads are cheap, so it earns far less per Indian viewer. Creators get a bigger share than on most rivals ({{REF:yt-share}}).

Moat, in plain words. Viewers come because creators are there, and creators stay because viewers and money are there. Twenty years of videos is a library nobody can copy. Google’s ad sales and Android’s reach sit behind it.

Comparison · Moj and Twitch

Moj (with ShareChat) rose after India banned TikTok in 2020 and has about 15 crore monthly users with ShareChat, across more than 15 Indian languages. It competes with Shorts and Reels by staying local: regional creators, regional ads, and micro-dramas of a few minutes. Twitch is the opposite shape: live, long and interactive, with chat as the product. It holds about half of the world’s live-streaming watch time, but live video is expensive to deliver; Twitch left South Korea in 2024 over network fees, and rival Kick pays creators 95% to lure them away.

Strengths

  • Largest library and creator base
  • Free, on every phone and TV
  • Pays creators best at scale

Weaknesses

  • Low earnings per Indian viewer
  • Policing harmful content at scale
  • Shorts earn less than long videos

2. Small creators in Bhojpuri, Odia or Assamese can’t reach the earning bar → auto-dubbing plus a fund for fast-growing channels in smaller languages → creators earning, by language.
