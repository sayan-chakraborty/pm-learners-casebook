Guesstimate 6.2 · Users of a Bhojpuri short-movie app GuesstimateHardIIM B Sigma PM Casebook 2026–27, p. 144
Try it first. Where do Bhojpuri speakers you know watch Bhojpuri films today, and do they pay? Estimate how many would use a new Bhojpuri-only app every month. Five minutes.
The question

Estimate the number of potential users for a Bhojpuri-language short-movie app.

A

I have a few clarifying questions. Are we estimating users in India only, or should I include the global Bhojpuri-speaking diaspora as well? And should I estimate everyone who speaks Bhojpuri, or narrow it down to those who could realistically use a smartphone app?

Q

Good questions. Include the diaspora, and yes, narrow it down to realistic users.

A

I’ll estimate this in three steps: first the total Bhojpuri-speaking population (TAM), then the portion with smartphones and regular data access (SAM), and finally the realistic share who’d actually adopt this app (SOM).

Is this approach fine?

Q

Sounds like a comprehensive approach, please proceed.

A

Starting with TAM, the total Bhojpuri-speaking population:

• Bihar population ~130 million → ~45% Bhojpuri-speaking → ~59 million

• Eastern UP population ~240 million → ~20-25% in the Bhojpuri belt → ~50 million

• Jharkhand border areas → ~5 million

• Global diaspora (Nepal, Gulf countries, Mauritius, Fiji, Caribbean) → ~6-8 million

Adding these up, TAM ≈ 120-125 million Bhojpuri speakers.

Does this breakdown seem reasonable?

Q

Yes, you may continue.

A

Not all speakers are potential app users, so I’ll apply two filters to narrow this to SAM: smartphone penetration and regular data usage.

• Smartphone penetration in the Bhojpuri belt: ~65%

• Regular data users among them: ~75%

SAM = 125 million × 0.65 × 0.75 ≈ 61 million reachable users.

Does this approach seem reasonable?

Q

Yes, that’s a fair filtering approach.

A

Now, for SOM, I’ll segment the reachable users by likelihood of actually trying the app:

• High-likelihood adopters urban migrant workers and youth: ~30% adoption

• Medium likelihood – Tier 2-4 families: ~15% adoption

• Low likelihood – NRI diaspora: ~10% adoption

Weighting across these segments gives a blended expected adoption rate of roughly 20-25% initially.

Q

That seems like a reasonable spread. Please go ahead and calculate the final number.

A

Taking the midpoint of ~22%:

SOM = 61 million × 0.22 ≈ 13-14 million

Total number of potential users for a Bhojpuri Short-film app = ~13-14 million.

Q

This was a comprehensive estimate. Thank you.

What worked · what was missing
  • Two sharp clarifying questions: India or the diaspora too, and all speakers or only realistic users.
  • A clean funnel from speakers to people online to people likely to adopt, with the sums shown.
  • Split adoption by segment rather than one guess for everyone.
  • “Users” never defined. People who try it once, or people who use it every month? 1.3 crore triers and 1.3 crore monthly users are very different businesses.
  • No free rival in the maths. Bhojpuri songs and films are already free on YouTube and Moj. A 22% adoption rate for a new app ignores that.
  • The blend isn’t shown. The 20–25% depends on how many of the 6.1 crore are youth, families or diaspora, and those shares were never given.

Card 1 mattered most: the interviewer asked for “potential users”, and the answer treated that as people who would adopt.

Check · “TAM ≈ 120–125 million Bhojpuri speakers” Directionally right

The 2011 Census counted about 5.1 crore people whose mother tongue is Bhojpuri; many more speak it but record Hindi. So 12 crore is a fair upper figure for people who understand Bhojpuri, and 5 crore a floor. The online filter (49%) is high for Bihar, which has among India’s fewest internet users per person (TRAI).

Stronger answer
A
1 · Unit

I’ll give two numbers: people who might try the app, and people who would use it every month once it is well known, say in its third year. Both include the diaspora.

Why this move: “potential users” is ambiguous; giving both numbers keeps the answer useful whichever was meant.

A
2 · Approach

Top-down from speakers: people who understand Bhojpuri → online → already watch short videos → would choose a Bhojpuri-only app over free options.

Why this move: the last step names the real competitor.

A
3–4 · Tree and numbers

About 9 crore people understand Bhojpuri (between the Census’s 5 crore and the candidate’s 12 crore). × 55% online = 5 crore. × 80% watch short videos every month = 4 crore. Of those, perhaps 25% would try a Bhojpuri app: about 1 crore triers. And 5–10% would use it every month despite YouTube and Moj: 20–40 lakh monthly users ({{REF:gtree62}}).

Why this move: the gap between trying (25%) and staying (5–10%) is the free rival at work.

A
5 · Check

ShareChat and Moj together have about 15 crore monthly users across more than 15 Indian languages, roughly 1 crore per language on average. A single-language, single-format app reaching 20–40 lakh monthly users sits sensibly below that average.

Why this move: a real, many-language app gives a ceiling per language.

A
6 · Range

About 1 crore people might try it; 20–40 lakh would use it monthly. The swing factor is the share who stay once the novelty wears off, which depends on having films you can’t find free elsewhere.

Why this move: it turns the estimate into a product insight: exclusive content decides the size.

How the answer hangs togetherDefining the user split the answer into triers and monthly users. The Census gave a floor for speakers, the free rival explained the drop from trying to staying, and the ShareChat–Moj average capped it. The range points at exclusive content, the one lever that would make a Bhojpuri app bigger.
The reusable trick

For any new app: people who could use it → online → already do this activity → try it → stay against the free option. Check against a similar app’s users per language or per city.
