Guesstimates: counting attention and fans

Media guesstimates come in two kinds. Money questions (“how much does YouTube earn a day?”) are really about time: ads are sold by the minute of attention, not by the person. Audience questions (“how many people would use this app?”) are about the difference between people who could use something and people who will, every month, when free rivals exist. The six cards still do the work.

1Pin down the unitAd revenue or all revenue? Downloads, tried once, or used every month?
2Pick the approachFor ad money, start from minutes watched; for users, from people who speak, own a phone, watch.
3Draw the treePeople → watching today → minutes → ads per minute → price per ad.
4Round numbers, one reason eachAds per minute and price per 1,000 differ by screen: Shorts, phone, TV.
5Check it another wayAgainst a published total (company results) and per person (does it sound like you?).
6Give a range, and what moves itUsually the price per 1,000 ads, or the share who would pick a new app over a free one.
Key idea · Ad money = minutes × ads per minute × price per ad

Counting “videos watched” undercounts ads: one 40-minute video can carry four mid-roll ads, and a 30-second Short often carries none. Start from minutes. For India, ₹ per 1,000 ad views is a small fraction of the US price, so the same minutes earn far less. That single fact explains most gaps between Indian and American media revenue.
