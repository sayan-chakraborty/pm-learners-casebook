Spotify: free music that sells Premium

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Ananya, 20, a college student in Pune, uses Spotify free. Every Monday she opens Discover Weekly, 30 songs picked from what she plays and what people with similar taste play. Most weeks she saves three or four. On the free plan an audio ad plays every few songs, and on her phone she can’t always pick the exact song she wants. In December, Wrapped tells her she was in the top 1% of listeners of an indie Hindi band; she shares it on Instagram, and two friends sign up. In January, fed up with ads before her exam playlist, she upgrades ({{REF:sp-flow}}).

Business model, with numbers. Premium subscriptions bring in most revenue; ads on the free tier bring the rest. In April–June 2026 Spotify had 77.7 crore monthly users and 30 crore paying, revenue of €4.8 bn and an operating profit of €655 mn. About two-thirds of revenue goes to labels and publishers, which is why its first full year of profit only came in 2024. The free tier is its marketing: most Premium subscribers started free.

Moat, in plain words. Years of your playlists and taste live here, which makes switching feel like losing your music. Its size gives it weight with labels. But the labels own the songs, and every rival has the same catalogue, so Spotify competes on picking, not on having.

Comparison · JioSaavn and the Indian music apps

In India, Spotify competes with YouTube Music (free with ads, and bundled with YouTube Premium), JioSaavn and Gaana. JioSaavn is strongest in Hindi and regional film music and reaches people through Jio: its Pro plan comes bundled with some recharges, and the casebook quotes about ₹99 a month for one person. Most Indian listeners never pay; the fight is for free listening hours, which earn from cheap Indian audio ads.

Strengths

  • Best-known personal playlists
  • Huge free funnel into Premium
  • Now steadily profitable

Weaknesses

  • Labels take two-thirds
  • Same songs as every rival
  • Few Indians pay for music

2. Families share one phone’s Premium → a cheaper family plan for two to three people in the same home → paying households; guardrail: individual plans moving down.
