Guesstimate 6.1 · YouTube’s daily ad revenue GuesstimateMediumIIM A Product Mindset 2026–27, p. 236
Try it first. How many minutes of YouTube did you watch yesterday, and how many ads did you see? Were you on a phone or a TV? Build one day of YouTube’s US ad revenue from that, for five minutes.
{{CASE:A236}}
What worked · what was missing
  • Scoped the unit (ad revenue only) and asked which market.
  • Wrote the equation first, and explained why demand, not server capacity, sets the number.
  • Removed views without ads (Premium, creators who don’t earn from ads) and checked against Google’s total.
  • Counted videos, not minutes. One ad per view undercounts long videos with mid-rolls and TV viewing, now YouTube’s biggest screen in the US.
  • Overlapping age bands: 13–24 and 24–44 both include 24-year-olds, and 0–5 at 0% ignores YouTube Kids.
  • The check was misread. $7.3 bn from the US out of $20–30 bn worldwide would make the US only a quarter to a third of YouTube, when the US brings in about half of Alphabet’s revenue. And YouTube’s ads alone were $36 bn in 2024.

Guesstimate kit check: 1 Unit ✓ · 2 Approach ✓ demand side · 3 Tree ✓ · 4 Numbers with reasons ✗ age rates unexplained · 5 Check ✗ done, but it should have raised a flag · 6 Range ✗. Card 5 again: the check is only useful if you let it change your answer.

Check · “Assuming USA gives $7.3B is a fair estimate” Doesn’t hold up

Alphabet reported YouTube ad revenue of $36.1 bn in 2024 and about $40 bn in 2025. It doesn’t split YouTube by country, but about half of Alphabet’s revenue comes from the US. Half of $40 bn is about $20 bn a year, or $20 bn ÷ 365 ≈ $55 million a day, more than twice the casebook’s $20 million.

Stronger answer
A
1 · Unit

YouTube’s ad revenue in the US on a typical day, including Shorts and viewing on TV screens; Premium and YouTube TV subscriptions are out.

Why this move: “revenue” could include subscriptions, which are large on their own. Saying what is in and out avoids an argument later.

A
2 · Approach

Start from minutes watched, because ads are placed by time: a long video carries several ads, a Short few or none.

Why this move: minutes match how the product actually earns, so the rates in the tree become ones you can defend.

A
3–4 · Tree and numbers

330 million Americans × 75% watch on a typical day ≈ 250 million viewers. × 60 minutes each = 15 billion minutes. × 85% not on Premium = 12.75 billion minutes with ads. × 1 ad every 4 minutes = 3.2 billion ad views. × $15 per 1,000 (cheap on Shorts, dear on TV) = about $48 million a day ({{REF:gtree61}}).

Why this move: each number has a reason: an hour is typical US viewing across phones and TVs, one ad per four minutes blends long videos and Shorts, and $15 averages cheap and expensive screens.

A
5 · Check

Against the company: YouTube ads were about $40 bn in 2025; if the US is roughly half, that is $20 bn ÷ 365 ≈ $55 million a day. Per person: $48 million ÷ 250 million viewers ≈ 19 cents a day, about $70 a year, which fits a market where Meta earns about $68 a quarter per user across all its apps. Both checks land near $50 million.

Why this move: a company total and a per-person figure attack the answer from two sides. When both agree, the estimate is safe.

A
6 · Range

$30–75 million a day, most likely about $50 million, or roughly $18 bn a year. The swing factors are ads per minute and the price per 1,000, which rises as more viewing moves to TV screens.

Why this move: the range tells the interviewer which two assumptions to argue about, which is more useful than a single number.

How the answer hangs togetherScoping to ad revenue fixed the unit. Starting from minutes matched how YouTube sells ads, which is why this answer is more than twice the casebook’s. The company total and the per-person check both point to about $50 million a day, and the range names ads per minute and TV prices as what would move it.
The reusable trick

Ad revenue = viewers × minutes × ads per minute × price per ad. Check it against the company’s annual figure divided by 365, and against revenue per person per day.
