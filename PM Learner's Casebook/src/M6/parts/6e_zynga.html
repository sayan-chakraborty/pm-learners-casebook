Zynga: free to play, paid for by a few

Walkthrough (illustrative persona). Lakshmi, 38, an accountant in Chennai, plays Toon Blast on the train home. She has played for two years and never paid. On level 212 she runs out of moves two cubes short. The game offers five more moves: watch a 30-second ad, or spend coins. She watches the ad and wins. Three seats away, a college student has spent ₹2,400 this month on boosters to beat a level before his friends. The game is the same for both. Its business needs Lakshmi happy and playing, because she makes the teams feel alive and fills ad slots, while the student and players like him pay most of the bills ({{REF:zy-flow}}).

Business model, with numbers. Free-to-play games earn from in-app purchases and ads. Usually only 2–5% of players ever pay, and a tiny group spends heavily. The test for every game is whether a player is worth more than it costs to win them ({{REF:zy-ltv}}). Zynga began with FarmVille on Facebook in 2009, grew through friends’ feeds and fell when Facebook cut those feeds; it then bought mobile studios with proven hits. Take-Two bought it in 2022 for $12.7 bn, and in the year to March 2026 mobile was about half of Take-Two’s $6.7 bn of net bookings (money players committed to spend).

Moat, in plain words. A portfolio of long-running hits, each with years of tuned levels and loyal teams; and the data to know which players will pay and which ads win players cheaply. Lesson from FarmVille: growth that depends on someone else’s platform can be switched off by that platform.

When it breaks · India’s ban on real-money games

The Online Gaming Act 2025 banned games played for money from 1 October 2025, including fantasy cricket and rummy, and banned their ads and payments. Casual games like Toon Blast, where you buy extras but can’t win money, were not affected. The line that matters is simple: can a player put money in and take money out? Any game design that looks like a bet now carries legal risk in India.

Strengths

  • Many long-lived hits
  • Skilled at buying players profitably
  • Take-Two’s money behind it

Weaknesses

  • Hits are rare and fade
  • Depends on app stores and ad networks
  • Heavy spenders invite scrutiny

2. Players leave when stuck on a hard level for days → spot players stuck three days and quietly give a free booster → day-30 return; guardrail: purchases per payer.
